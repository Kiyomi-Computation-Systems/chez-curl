M5 builds `(http-client)`, the high level, on `(curl)` alone (ADR-0007). After M5 a caller can:

- open a session, which owns an engine and the defaults its calls share, and know it is closed
  when `call-with-http-session` returns or escapes;
- call `http-get` and its siblings, and get back a response, with a body made from bytes, a
  string or a form, and params added to the URL (ADR-0025);
- have redirects followed by chez-curl's rules, which hold alike on every libcurl, and leave only
  `curl_url`'s own variance (§2, §3.4);
- have a failure retried, when the session or the call names a policy (ADR-0026);
- run many calls at once from one thread, in a request group that does not return while one is
  live, or with `http-parallel`.

- **The low level keeps its stand,** no policy (brief §6.1). It gains four URL functions the
  high level needs (§3.1), and `curl-token-outcome`, a done token's outcome as data, which
  slice 3's review showed the drive needs (ADR-0021, amended 2026-10-28), and
  `curl-request-check`, a submit's checks without the submit, which slice 4's review showed
  `http-parallel` needs (ADR-0023, amended 2026-10-28), and nothing else.
- **No thread per request** (ADR-0012). A call blocks its caller's thread, and a group
  multiplexes its calls on one thread through a mailbox (ADR-0021).
- **Streaming, `call-with-response-port` among it, is M6's;** cookies, auth and a proxy given by
  URL are M7's (brief §8).
- **M5 has four slices:** the session and the verbs; redirects; retries; the request group and
  `http-parallel`.

## 2. Measured before planning (2026-10-27)

- **`CURLINFO_REDIRECT_URL`, against the first `Location` resolved by `curl_url_set`**
  ([`research/m5/redirect-url/`](research/m5/redirect-url/)). A C probe made one transfer with
  the options the engine sets, its URL through `CURLOPT_CURLU`, the pins, and `FOLLOWLOCATION`
  off, through `curl_easy_perform`, against a `node:net` server that wrote each head byte for
  byte, on 7.85.0, Ubuntu's 8.5.0 with OpenSSL and with GnuTLS, Guix's 8.20.0 and 8.23.0: 62
  cases each, the two 8.5.0 builds alike (ADR-0008, amended 2026-10-27).

  | Case | 7.85.0 | 8.5.0 | 8.20.0 | 8.23.0 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | libcurl's URL for a `Location` on 200, 201, 299 or 400 | none | none | given | given |
  | credentials in libcurl's URL, the request's or the `Location`'s | kept | kept | kept | dropped |
  | `Location: /x<0x01>y`, by libcurl | raw | raw | `/x%01y` | `/x%01y` |
  | the same, by `curl_url_set` | refused, 25, `BAD_PATH` | refused, 3, `MALFORMED_INPUT` | `/x%01y` | `/x%01y` |
  | `Location: javascript:alert(1)`, by libcurl | resolved as a path | raw | raw | raw |
  | `Location: #only`, both | last segment and query dropped | the same | kept | kept |
  | a byte above 0x7F in a path, both | `%e9` | `%e9` | `%E9` | `%E9` |
  | two `Location` headers | the first | the first | fails, 8 | fails, 8 |
  | a 110,000-byte `Location` | fails, 27 | fails, 27 | fails, 100 | fails, 100 |

  On all five: where libcurl's answer is a parsed URL, it equals `curl_url_set`'s resolution,
  but for 8.23.0's credentials; libcurl hands back `http://[::1/x`, a port of 99999 and
  `foo://bar/x` as they came, which `curl_url_set` refuses; a UTF-8 host comes back raw from
  both; an empty or blank `Location` gives none; and no version gives a reference without a
  fragment the request's own, as RFC 9110 §10.2.2 asks. `EFFECTIVE_URL` drops the request's
  credentials on 8.5.0 and 8.23.0, and keeps them on 7.85.0 and 8.20.0. On 8.5.0,
  `curl_easy_header` gives an empty or blank `Location`'s value as a lone CR, 0x0D; 7.85.0,
  8.20.0 and 8.23.0 give it empty. The Mac's 8.7.1, measured after the plan merged, answers as
  8.5.0 on 56 of the 62, and the other six three ways: an empty `Location` read as empty, 100 for the
  110,000 bytes, and `EFFECTIVE_URL` keeping the credentials (ADR-0008, amended).

  **So M5 follows a redirect from the first `Location` header's bytes,** resolved by ADR-0008's
  decision 4 against the URL it requested, never from `CURLINFO_REDIRECT_URL`
  (ADR-0023, amended 2026-10-27). Which statuses redirect, which credentials carry, and what is
  refused are then chez-curl's rules, alike on every libcurl. What stays is `curl_url`'s own
  variance, which `test-url` pins by version, and §3.4 takes the one case of it that would
  fetch another resource, a lone `#`, out of `curl_url`'s hands.
- **Measured by the plan's review** ([`research/m5/review/`](research/m5/review/)), in the
  container on 8.20.0, and rerun there:
  - a Latin-1 byte in a host or in the user info comes back raw from `curl_url_get`, whole and
    by part; in the path, query and fragment it comes back as `%E9`. `get-part` decodes with
    `utf8->string`, which reads `caf\xe9` and `caf\xe8` as one string, so a request would go to
    a host no one named;
  - Chez's `random` draws the same sequence in every process, from a fixed `random-seed`; a
    `make-pseudo-random-generator` draws differently in each, since Chez seeds one from the
    UTC time, `(time-second + time-nanosecond) & 0xFFFFFFFF` (its `s/5_3.ss`, read by slice 3's
    review): time-seeded, not from entropy.
- **Read in libcurl's source by the review,** not measured:
  - `Curl_retry_request` resends a request of any method, a `POST` and its body among them, on a
    fresh connection when a reused one died with no response byte, up to 5 times (7.85.0's
    `lib/transfer.c` lines 1850 to 1896; 8.23.0's, 600 to 648);
  - libcurl's own follow drops a custom `Host` on a change of host (7.85.0's `lib/http.c`, lines
    2100 to 2112).
- **What a retry reads was measured in M4** (ADR-0022, amended): the class follows the code,
  and the request size is 0 exactly when nothing was sent, `#f` on 8.7.0 and 8.7.1.

## 3. Decisions that bind every slice

### 3.1 The public surface

- **`(http-client)` exports, by the end of M5:**
  - the session: `make-http-session`, `http-session?`, `http-session-close` and
    `call-with-http-session` (§3.2);
  - the verbs: `http-request`, `http-get`, `http-head`, `http-post`, `http-put`, `http-patch`
    and `http-delete` (ADR-0025);
  - the response: `http-response?` and its accessors, `http-response-header` and
    `http-response-text` (§3.3);
  - the retry policy: `make-http-retry`, `http-retry?` (§3.5);
  - the group: `call-with-request-group`, `http-await`, `http-pending?`, `http-pending-done?`,
    `http-pending-cancel`, and `http-parallel` (§3.7);
  - its conditions (§3.3), with their predicates and accessors.
- **It re-exports none of `(curl)`.** A caller who catches a class imports `(curl)` too, or
  catches `&curl-error`, which every high-level condition is under (§3.3).
- **`(curl)` gains four URL functions,** each a `CURLU` made and freed within the call, on the
  calling thread, as `url-resolve` is now (ADR-0008):
  - `curl-url-append-query : String String -> String`: the URL with the query part, which comes
    encoded, appended after a `&` unless the query is empty or ends in one; written from what
    `curl_url_get` gives back, then parsed whole, and its query read back, or the part refused
    as `query-not-kept` (ADR-0028, slice 1). Setting a part changes its hex case by version,
    where a whole URL keeps it (§4.1);
  - `curl-url-part : String Symbol -> (U String Natural #f)`: one part of the parsed URL,
    `url`, `scheme`, `user`, `password`, `host`, `port`, `path`, `query`, `fragment` or
    `zoneid`, `#f` for one it lacks; the port by `CURLU_DEFAULT_PORT`, a natural (slice 1);
    `zoneid`, an IPv6 host's zone, which the host leaves out (slice 2's review);
  - `curl-url-resolve : String (U String Bytevector) -> String`: the reference resolved against
    the base by decision 4. A string is a URL as `make-curl-request` takes one, sent as UTF-8; a
    bytevector, a header's Latin-1 as bytes, is encoded first, as `curl-url-with-fragment`
    encodes one (ADR-0029, amended, slice 2): a raw byte above 0x7F in an absolute URL came
    back raw, refused as `not-utf-8`, where a relative reference's came back encoded;
  - `curl-url-with-fragment : String (U String Bytevector #f) -> String`: the URL with its
    fragment replaced, or cleared by `#f` or an empty one, for §3.4's two fragment rules:
    written from what `curl_url_get` gives back, then parsed whole. A bytevector, a header's
    bytes, is encoded first, each byte at or below 0x20, 0x7F and each above as upper-case
    `%XX`; a string comes as a URL holds it (ADR-0029, slice 2). Setting the part stores its
    bytes unchecked, and moves its hex case by version (§5.1).
- **`(curl)` gains `curl-request-check : Request -> Void` too** (slice 4; ADR-0023, amended
  2026-10-28): the checks `curl-submit` runs on its caller's thread, the URL's parse and scheme
  and the CA file against the environment (ADR-0024) among them, raising what they raise, a
  refusal's who naming `curl-request-check`; it copies no body and queues nothing.
  `http-parallel` runs it on every spec before any submit.
- **Every answer is UTF-8, or refused:** an answer whose bytes are not UTF-8 raises
  `&curl-url-error`, a new reason, `not-utf-8`, never a string decoded with U+FFFD (§2). The
  existing `url-normalize` and `url-resolve` take the same rule.
- **A bytevector reference is no exception to the UTF-8 rule:** this plan had it hand libcurl
  its bytes as the rule's second exception, beside header strings; ADR-0029's amendment encodes
  it into ASCII first, so the rule keeps its one exception. A 0x00 byte in one is refused, as
  `embedded-nul`, and the condition's input holds the bytes read as Latin-1.
- **Every export carries a Typed Racket style signature** (AGENTS.md), and `(http-client)`
  names each condition's reasons.

### 3.2 Sessions and calls (ADR-0025)

- **`(make-http-session KEY VALUE ...)`** makes an engine and holds the defaults its calls
  share:
  - the request's keys but `method` and `body` (ADR-0023): `headers`, `user-agent`,
    `timeout-ms`, `connect-timeout-ms`, `body-limit`, `ca-file`, `verify` and `proxy`, each
    with the low level's default;
  - `redirects`: a natural, the most a call follows, or `#f` to follow none; 20, after the
    browsers';
  - `allow-downgrade`: whether a redirect from `https` to `http` is followed; `#f`
    (ADR-0027);
  - `retry`: a policy, or `#f`; `#f` (ADR-0026).

  An unknown key, a repeated key and a key with no value are refused by name, as the low level
  refuses its own. Each value's shape and range are checked when the session is made, by
  building a request from the defaults, as `make-curl-request` checks them. A URL's parse and
  the environment's refusals (ADR-0024) stay where the low level makes them, at a call's submit.
- **`(http-session-close S)`** stops its engine (ADR-0012): every call in flight ends as
  stopped, and a later call raises the engine's `&curl-engine-error`. A second close returns.
  Close does not drain: a drain is the caller's, an await, then the close (ADR-0012, amended
  2026-10-24).
- **`(call-with-http-session KEY VALUE ... PROC)`** makes a session, calls PROC with it, and
  closes it when PROC returns or escapes, by `dynamic-wind`.
- **A session may be shared by threads.** Beside its engine, its one mutable part is its jitter
  generator, held under a mutex of its own (§3.5).
- **`(http-request S METHOD URL KEY VALUE ...)`,** and `(http-get S URL KEY VALUE ...)` and its
  siblings, each the method its name says. The keys are the session's, each replacing the
  session's value, and three more:
  - `body`: a bytevector, or a string, sent as its UTF-8 bytes, with no `Content-Type` of its
    own;
  - `form`: a list of name and value string pairs, encoded (below), with `Content-Type:
    application/x-www-form-urlencoded` unless the merged headers name a `Content-Type`;
  - `params`: a list of name and value string pairs, encoded (below), and appended to the URL's
    query by `curl-url-append-query`.

  `body` and `form` together are refused, and so is either with `get` or `head`.
- **The merge,** a procedure the suite tests on its own:
  - headers merge by name, A to Z folded alone (`traps.md`): a call's header replaces every
    session header of its name; the rest of the session's come first, in order;
  - a field and a header of the same thing, the call's wins: a call's `User-Agent` header
    replaces the session's `user-agent`, and a call's `user-agent` drops the session's
    `User-Agent` header, which the low level would otherwise refuse as `duplicates-field`.
- **The form encoding** is the WHATWG URL Standard's `application/x-www-form-urlencoded`
  serializer, on UTF-8 bytes: `*`, `-`, `.`, `_`, and ASCII letters and digits are kept, a space
  becomes `+`, every other byte `%XX`, upper-case hex. Names and values alike; `=` between, `&`
  between pairs, in the order given. A Chez string holds no lone surrogate, so every string has
  UTF-8 bytes. The suite tests every char below U+0100 and one of each UTF-8 length, against
  the whole complement of the kept set (`traps.md`).
- **A call's `timeout-ms` bounds the whole call:**
  - every transfer, every redirect and retry, and every wait between, from the call's start; a
    call waiting for a group's slot is already on its clock (§3.7);
  - each transfer is submitted with the time left, rounded up to a whole millisecond, as its
    own `timeout-ms`; with none left, the call raises `&http-timeout` before it submits;
  - `connect-timeout-ms` stays each transfer's own;
  - it is libcurl's bound on each transfer, never a promise: the engine's queue, the floor's
    stalled teardown (ADR-0019) and a late 28, 1,014 ms once (ADR-0019, amended 2026-10-25), can
    each let a call overrun it.

### 3.3 The response, and the conditions

- **`http-response`,** read from the last transfer's `curl-response`: `status`,
  `http-version`, `url`, `headers`, `body` and `timings`, then `history`: the earlier
  responses of the call, oldest first, each with an empty history.
  - `url` is the URL the high level requested for the last transfer, as `curl-url-part` gives
    it whole: alike on every libcurl, unlike `EFFECTIVE_URL` (§2). It holds that URL's user
    info and fragment, if it has them;
  - `(http-response-header R NAME)`: the first header of NAME, A to Z folded, or `#f`;
  - `(http-response-text R)`: the body decoded as UTF-8, a malformed sequence read as U+FFFD,
    as `utf8->string` does, which also drops a leading byte-order mark. It reads no charset.
- **A status is data.** No status raises; a redirect the rules refuse does (§3.4).
- **Conditions,** each under `&http-error`, itself under `(curl)`'s `&curl-error`:
  - `&http-request-error`: a key or a value of the high level's refused, with `field`, `value`
    and `reason`, as the low level's `&curl-request-error` has. A request key's value is
    checked by the low level, whose condition passes through, compounded with `&http-exchange`;
  - `&http-redirect-error`: `reason`, `too-many-redirects`, `bad-location` or
    `insecure-redirect`; the `response` that could not be followed; and the `location`, the
    header's string, or `#f`. A `bad-location` that `curl_url_set` refused is compounded with
    its `&curl-url-error`; one whose scheme is not `http` or `https` has none;
  - `&http-timeout`: the call's bound passed. When libcurl's 28 was the bound's (§3.5), its
    `&curl-timeout` is compounded with it, so both predicates hold;
  - `&http-group-error`: `reason` `wrong-thread`, `closed` or `cancelled` (§3.7).
- **What a call raises carries its context:** every failure of the call's own, the low level's
  included, is compounded with `&http-exchange`: the call's method and URL, the history so far,
  and the number of transfers made. The URL holds the user info the caller gave. R6RS compound
  conditions keep every component's predicate; the review's probe built one under `&curl-error`
  in the container. A condition the caller's own interrupt handler raises during the drive
  leaves as it was raised, continuably, so a handler that returns resumes the call: slice 1
  first compounded those too, which killed a resumable `&warning` and could stack a second
  `&http-exchange` (its scoped review). One that lands while the keys and the URL are checked is
  still compounded: that guard must take the low level's assertion violations, which are no
  `&curl-error`.

### 3.4 Redirects

- **Followed:** 301, 302, 303, 307 and 308, with a `Location`. Any other status, 300, 304, 305
  and 306 among them, is answered.
- **The `Location`:** the first header of that name, its Latin-1 string's bytes, encoded and
  resolved by `curl-url-resolve` against the URL the transfer requested (ADR-0008, amended
  2026-10-27; ADR-0029, amended 2026-10-28).
  - An empty or blank one, spaces, tabs and the lone CR 8.5.0's header API gives for one (§2),
    is no redirect: the response is answered, as libcurl answers it.
  - A reference that is `#` and a fragment alone keeps the URL and sets its fragment, by
    `curl-url-with-fragment`. `curl_url`'s resolution would drop the last segment and the query
    on 7.85.0, 8.5.0 and 8.7.1 (`test-url`'s `#s` rows), and fetch another resource. On 8.20.0
    and 8.23.0 it would raise hex given in lower case, so the rule's test holds `%e9`.
  - One `curl_url_set` refuses, or one whose scheme is not `http` or `https`, or one whose
    answer is not UTF-8, raises `&http-redirect-error`, `bad-location`.
  - `curl_url`'s other variance stays, each pinned by version: `javascript:alert(1)` and `g:h`
    are followed as a path on 7.85.0; a bad IPv6 host is refused with 4 there and 22 after;
    and the hex case of `%XX` given in a relative reference. Bytes 0x01 and 0x7F, which 7.85.0
    and 8.5.0 refused raw, are followed encoded on every libcurl (ADR-0029, amended).
  - Two `Location` headers fail the transfer with 8 on 8.20.0 and 8.23.0 (§2), a low-level
    `&curl-protocol-error`; on 7.85.0 and 8.5.0 the first is followed. The suite pins both.
  - A reference with no fragment takes the URL's own (RFC 9110 §10.2.2), by
    `curl-url-with-fragment`. It is never sent, and shows only in `http-response-url`.
- **The method and the body** (RFC 9110 §15.4):
  - 301 and 302: a `post` becomes a `get`, without its body; every other method is kept, with
    its body. libcurl drops a custom method's body there (the M4 plan, §2): chez-curl does not;
  - 303: every method but `head` becomes a `get`, without its body;
  - 307 and 308: the method and the body are kept.

  When the body is dropped, so is every header the call named that is specific to it: each
  whose name begins `Content-`, and `Digest` and `Last-Modified` (RFC 9110 §15.4, item 5), and
  `Expect`, whose `100-continue` a client must not send with no body (RFC 9110 §10.1.1, the
  slice's review), A to Z folded.
- **Across origins** (ADR-0008: scheme, host with A to Z folded, its zone ID, and port by
  `CURLU_DEFAULT_PORT`, each from `curl-url-part`, compared on strings that passed the UTF-8
  rule; libcurl gives an IPv6 host without its zone, ADR-0008, amended 2026-10-28): a redirect
  to another origin drops, for every later transfer of the call, the call's and the session's
  - `Authorization` and `Cookie`;
  - `Proxy-Authorization`: with `HEADEROPT` at its default, libcurl sends `HTTPHEADER` to the
    origin inside a CONNECT tunnel as well (7.85.0's `lib/http.c`, lines 1810 to 1818, read, not
    measured), so a caller's would reach every origin a redirect names;
  - and, on any change of host or of its zone, `Host`, as libcurl's own follow does (§2).

  Every other header follows, an `X-Api-Key` among them: README.org will say so. The URL's own
  credentials follow RFC 3986's resolution: a relative reference keeps the URL's, and an
  absolute one carries its own or none.
- **A downgrade** (ADR-0027): a redirect from `https` to `http` raises `&http-redirect-error`,
  `insecure-redirect`, unless `allow-downgrade` is `#t`, when it is followed as any change of
  origin is. `http` to `https` is followed.
- **The bound:** `redirects` N follows N at most; the next redirect raises
  `&http-redirect-error`, `too-many-redirects`. `#f` follows none, and answers the redirect.
  A loop is caught by the bound.
- **Each transfer of a redirect** is a new request, made by `make-curl-request` from the new
  URL with every other key read from the last request's accessors: `url` is no key of
  `curl-request-with`, which keeps a request's URL (ADR-0023). The time left is its
  `timeout-ms`.

### 3.5 Retries (ADR-0026)

- **`(make-http-retry KEY VALUE ...)`** makes a policy, data alone, with:
  - `attempts`: transfers per step, at least 1; 3;
  - `backoff-ms`, `max-backoff-ms`: the first wait, doubling to the cap; 200 and 10,000;
  - `jitter`: `#t` waits a uniform time up to the backoff ("full jitter"), `#f` the backoff
    itself; `#t`;
  - `statuses`: the statuses retried, each from 400 to 599 (ADR-0026, amended 2026-10-28); 429
    and 503;
  - `methods`: the methods retried after a request was sent; `get`, `head`, `put` and
    `delete`, which RFC 9110 §9.2.2 calls idempotent.
- **Off means chez-curl adds no attempt;** libcurl may still resend. It resends a request of
  any method, a `post` among them, when a reused connection died before any response byte, up
  to 5 times (§2), and the request size sums those sends. M5 offers no at-most-once mode, one
  that never reuses a connection; it is carried (§10).
- **Retried, while attempts remain and the wait ends before the call's bound:**
  - a failure, `perform-failed`, of class `&curl-connect-error`, `&curl-send-receive-error`, or
    `&curl-timeout` before the call's bound, with request size 0, for any method: nothing was
    sent;
  - the same with a request size above 0, or `#f`, unknown, for a method in `methods` alone.
    On 8.7.0 and 8.7.1, the Mac's among them, every transfer that ran carries `#f` (ADR-0022,
    amended 2026-10-27), so a method outside `methods`, a `post`, is never retried there;
  - a response whose status is in `statuses`, for a method in `methods`.
- **A 28 is the call's bound's** when `connect-timeout-ms` is not shorter than the time left,
  since each transfer's `timeout-ms` is the time left; otherwise `exchange-step` compares the
  monotonic time with the bound. A call with a bound and no `connect-timeout-ms` submits each
  transfer with the time left as its connect timeout too, or 300,000 ms when less: libcurl's
  connect takes that default, `DEFAULT_CONNECT_TIMEOUT`, when none is set (7.85.0's
  `lib/connect.c`, read by slice 1's review; 8.23.0 defines the same constant), so with more
  than 300 s left its 28 is not the bound's, and the cap keeps a bounded call's connect no longer
  than an unbounded one's. The time is read once the await notices the token done, up to about
  10 ms after it ended (the review measured a median of 8.8 ms): a transfer's own connect
  timeout that fires that close to the deadline is reported as the bound's. With size above 0 a 28 before the bound comes
  only after libcurl's own resend, on a fresh connection's connect.
- **`Retry-After`,** on a retried status, sets the wait in place of the backoff: delta-seconds,
  or a date in any of RFC 9110's three forms, IMF-fixdate, RFC 850 and asctime, which a
  recipient must accept (§5.6.7), measured against the UTC time; an RFC 850 two-digit year
  more than 50 years in the future, by its timestamp, is the century before, as §5.6.7
  requires (slice 3's review). One that cannot be read is
  taken as absent, and the backoff waits. One longer than `max-backoff-ms` or than the time
  left answers the response. Jitter never shortens it: it draws on the backoff alone.
- **Never retried:** a TLS, protocol or too-large class, a code no class names, any other
  reason, an engine's stop, a refusal, a redirect error, and a 28 at the call's bound.
- **After the last attempt** a failure raises, and a status is answered, as with no policy.
- **When the call's bound passes during a retry's wait or its next transfer, and the retry sent
  nothing,** the call ends as it would with no policy: it answers the retried response, or
  raises the retried failure, without `&http-timeout`. Nothing sent means the submission found no
  time left, or the bound's 28 came with a request size of 0; a 28 with a size above 0, or `#f`,
  raises with `&http-timeout`, as without a policy (ADR-0026, amended twice 2026-10-28, after
  slice 3's reviews). A redirect clears the step's retried outcome.
- **A step is one URL:** a redirect starts the count again, so a call makes at most
  `attempts` × (`redirects` + 1) transfers of its own.
- **Jitter draws from the session's own generator,** a `make-pseudo-random-generator`, under the
  session's mutex, never from Chez's `random`, which draws the same sequence in every process
  (§2). The draw, in [0, 1), is an argument of `exchange-step`.
- **The wait, on the caller's thread,** is a take from an empty private mailbox, bounded by the
  wait's length: `curl-mailbox-take` polls in steps of 10 ms at most, refuses to start inside a
  critical section, and answers Ctrl-C, as every wait does (ADR-0013, amended 2026-10-25). The
  high level cannot ask whether interrupts are on itself: `disable-interrupts` is the engine's
  alone (check-seam). In a group, a wait is a due time the group's loop keeps (§3.7).

### 3.6 The exchange: a functional core and its shell

- **An exchange is one call:** the first request, the request of the step in flight, its count
  of attempts, the history, the bound, and the headers that are dropped from here on.
- **`exchange-step`** takes the state, an outcome, a response or a condition, the monotonic time
  in nanoseconds, for the bound, the UTC time in seconds, for a `Retry-After` date, and a draw
  in [0, 1). It answers one of `(done RESPONSE)`, `(fail CONDITION)`, or
  `(next STATE REQUEST WAIT-MS)`. It makes no transfer, reads no clock and draws no number, so
  the suite drives it by tables. It calls `(curl)`'s URL functions, whose answers depend on the
  loaded libcurl alone.
- **The shell for one call** (slice 1), on its caller's thread: submit, await, step, wait, and
  again.
  - It submits and records the token in one critical section, so an interrupt handler that
    escapes cannot fall between, and leave a live transfer the cancel cannot see. A submit never
    waits, but it copies the body there: about 50 ms for 64 MB, and 207 to 227 ms for 256 MB, for
    which Ctrl-C and every other thread's collections wait
    ([research](research/m5/submit-time/)).
  - An escape from the await cancels the recorded token, by a critical `dynamic-wind`, before it
    leaves: a second escape that lands in a plain one's after thunk skipped the cancel
    ([research](research/m5/escape/)). A call inside a critical section is refused before its
    submit. A Chez engine's expiry is an escape too: a call in an engine whose fuel runs out in
    its await has its transfer cancelled, and raises `cancelled` once the engine resumes.
- **The shell for a group** (slice 4): §3.7.

### 3.7 The request group and `http-parallel`

- **`(call-with-request-group S KEY VALUE ... PROC)`** calls PROC with `go`. Its one key is
  `limit`, a positive integer or `#f`, the most calls in flight; `#f`.
  - `(go METHOD URL KEY VALUE ...)` starts a call, as `http-request` takes one, and answers a
    pending call at once. A call past the limit waits its turn, on its clock (§3.2), and a call
    in a retry's wait keeps its slot.
  - `(http-await P)` answers its response, or raises what it raised.
  - `(http-pending-done? P)` runs one turn of the group's loop, with no wait, then answers
    whether the call has ended. So a caller that polls it moves the group on.
  - `(http-pending-cancel P)` ends the call: its live token is cancelled, or its waiting step
    dropped. Its await raises `&http-group-error`, `cancelled`, compounded with the low level's
    condition when a transfer was live.
- **A group's calls share one mailbox,** and the thread that awaits drives them all, so a
  redirect or a retry of one call proceeds while another is awaited.
  - **Each token is stepped once.** The loop keeps the set of its live tokens not yet stepped. It
    steps a token only while it is in the set, and takes it out in the same step; a notice for a
    token outside the set is dropped. A notice can be lost to an escape (ADR-0021, amended), or
    arrive after a look found the token done: either way, the token is stepped once.
  - **Each turn** reads `curl-token-done?` of every token in the set, and steps those done; then
    takes, bounded by the next due time and by 100 ms, so a lost notice is found within one
    bound.
  - **Each submit** records its token in the same critical section (§3.6). A submit the loop
    makes for one call, a redirect, a retry or a call let in under the limit, that raises, the
    engine stopped, a URL refused, the environment's refusal, is that call's failure, and never
    leaves the await of another.
- **A group is its thread's:** `go`, `http-await`, `http-pending-done?` and
  `http-pending-cancel` from another thread raise `&http-group-error`, `wrong-thread`, and
  `go` after PROC has returned raises `closed`.
- **The join:** the group does not return while one of its calls is live.
  - PROC returns: the group awaits every call, and answers PROC's values. A call that failed
    and was never awaited raises then, the first to fail first; a call the caller cancelled
    raises nothing there.
  - PROC raises or escapes: the group cancels every live token, drops every waiting step, waits
    for every token to end, and then lets the raise leave.
  - The join can wait: a cancel ends a token only once `curl_multi_remove_handle` returns, and
    on 7.85.0 to 7.86.x, with no `QUICK_EXIT`, a stalled lookup holds that up to glibc's
    resolver timeout (ADR-0019). A second Ctrl-C during the join abandons it, and leaves those
    tokens live. Both are documented, not hidden.
- **`(http-parallel S SPECS KEY VALUE ...)`:** each spec a list `(METHOD URL KEY VALUE ...)`;
  its one key `limit`, as a group's. Every spec's keys, then its first request by
  `curl-request-check`, a submit's own checks, its URL's parse and scheme and the
  environment's refusal among them, are checked before any is submitted (ADR-0023, amended
  2026-10-28; this plan first had `curl-url-part` read the scheme, and let the environment's
  refusal come mid-batch). A refusal a submit makes still comes mid-batch only from what the
  check cannot see: the engine stopped or failed. It answers the
  responses in SPECS' order. The first call to fail raises, once the rest are cancelled and
  ended.
- **ADR-0012's test has no high-level half to write:** the high level registers no callback,
  and `(curl)` runs none of its code on the engine's thread, so a check of a step's thread
  could never fail. The low level's delivery test holds the rule.
- **As built (slice 4), the gaps above filled by its brief and its three reviews:**
  - **`go` checks first,** as `http-request` does: a refusal of its keys raises from `go`,
    compounded with `&http-exchange`, and makes no call. From its first submit on, what becomes
    of a call is its own answer.
  - **A slot is held from a call's first submit until it ends,** through every redirect's and
    retry's submit, and a retry's wait, none of which waits for one. A queued call's deadline is
    a due time: once past, the call fails with `&http-timeout`, with no slot taken.
  - **Every change to the group's record is one critical section that waits for nothing but a
    mutex's brief hold:** a step with what it submits, a due submit, an expiry, each admission,
    each entry, a cancel. A guard inside each call's section makes whatever that call's own step
    or submit raises its failure, so no raise strands it where the join would wait for good
    (the first review).
  - **An await answers the same any number of times,** after its group has returned too. A
    cancel of an ended call changes nothing but this: a failed one is exempt from the join's
    raise, whatever the turns saw of it (the first review).
  - **The join is two winds:** an inner critical one, whose after thunk closes the group, cancels
    every live token and ends every unended call, and an outer plain one, whose after thunk
    waits for every token to end.
  - **An escape closes the group for good,** a Chez engine's expiry among them (the second
    review): a resumed `go` that had not entered its call raises `closed`, once its entry's
    section has ended, so a handler around it runs with interrupts on (the third review); one
    resumed after its entry answers its pending, whose await raises `cancelled`; and a resumed
    `http-parallel` enters no more calls, and answers responses only when every call is done;
    otherwise it raises the first failure, else the cancel of the first call in SPECS' order
    that is not done. An expiry inside a call's checks leaves `(curl)`'s URL parse behind, so
    the resumed call raises its re-entry refusal (§10, carried).
  - **`call-with-request-group`, `http-await`, `http-pending-done?` and `http-parallel` refuse
    inside a critical section,** as `http-request` does; `go` and `http-pending-cancel` do not.
  - **The first to fail** is the first the group saw fail, ties in the order of their latest
    submits.
  - **The plan's bound of 2 s for 20 calls holds under the memory gate,** 553 to 764 ms there on
    the final tree ([research](research/m5/group/timing/); §7.1), so no suite asks whether it
    runs under Valgrind.

### 3.8 The seam and the conventions

- **check-seam:** each `(http-client ...)` library imports `(curl)` first and nothing else from
  the low level (ADR-0007), which the seam already holds. `sleep`, `make-time`, `current-time`,
  `time-second`, `time-nanosecond`, `get-thread-id`, `dynamic-wind`, `critical-section`,
  `make-mutex` and `with-mutex` are in `allowed-chez-names` already. Jitter needs
  `make-pseudo-random-generator` and `pseudo-random-generator-next!`, reviewed additions for the
  high level's libraries alone, in `library-only-chez-names`, never `random` (§3.5); slice 3
  adds them, and test-check-seam's list written out.
- **No native code in the high level:** no `(curl private ...)` import, so nothing it makes is
  counted, and the counters gate holds at zero by the low level's own checks.
- **The memory gate** runs the four new suites, as every suite.
- **Every raw-face check makes its own session:** the raw face closes after each script without
  saying so, and curl 8.23.0 resends on each dead pooled connection, then fails with 55
  (`traps.md`, ADR-0020).
