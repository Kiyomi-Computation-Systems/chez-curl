Redirects and retries

(http-client) follows redirects by its own rules, not libcurl's, and retries only by a policy you give. Each request a redirect or a retry makes is a transfer of its own.

The examples on this page call the test server: start it first, with node tests/origin.mjs, and give an example the address after "http": in the line it prints, not raw's or h2c's (The examples).

(call-with-http-session 'timeout-ms 10000
                        'retry (make-http-retry 'attempts 3 'backoff-ms 10 'statuses '(503))
  (lambda (session)
    ;; A redirect is followed; the 302 joins the response's history.
    (let ((response (http-get session (string-append base "/redirect/302?to=/status/200"))))
      (show (http-response-status response) " "
            (map http-response-status (http-response-history response))))
    ;; The server answers this URL 503 twice, then 200.
    (let ((url (string-append base "/parts?key=" (unseen-key session "example-retry-")
                              "&fail=2")))
      ;; A call's retry #f overrides the session's policy: the 503 is answered.
      (show (http-response-status (http-get session url 'retry #f)))
      ;; With the policy, the second 503 is retried, and the 200 after it is
      ;; answered. A retried response joins no history.
      (let ((response (http-get session url)))
        (show (http-response-status response) " " (http-response-history response))))))
200 (302)
503
200 ()

The test server's /parts?key=K&fail=2 answers 503 to K's first two requests, and counts each key for as long as it runs: unseen-key picks one it has never counted, so every run of the example prints the same.

Redirects

A call follows a 301, 302, 303, 307 or 308 whose first Location is neither empty nor blank, up to redirects times: 20, unless the session or the call says otherwise. #f follows none, and answers the redirect. Each response it followed joins the history, oldest first.

  • The Location is resolved against the URL the transfer asked for. One that is # and a fragment keeps that URL with the new fragment; one with no # keeps that URL's own fragment.
  • To another origin, another scheme, host or port, the call drops Authorization, Cookie and its credentials, and Host when the host changes. A header dropped stays dropped, even back on the first origin. Every other header follows, so an X-Api-Key reaches the new origin too. No request holds a Proxy-Authorization header: it is refused when you make one.
  • A user:pass@ in the URL stays for a Location that is a path or a query. One that names a host, //host/p or a whole URL, carries its own, or none.
  • From https to http is refused, unless allow-downgrade is #t.
  • A 303 makes a get of anything but a head, and so do a 301 and a 302 of a post. The body goes, and with it your Content- headers, Digest, Last-Modified and Expect.
  • Every other key follows: the HTTP version, the proxy and its credentials, and a session's cookie jar, to another origin too. So the next request can be refused as any request would be: prior-knowledge-over-https for an https:// one by prior knowledge, credentials-beside-userinfo for one within the origin to a URL holding a user, proxy-tls-in-tls.
  • A producer's body is never sent twice. Once libcurl has asked for it, a 307 or 308, or a 301 or 302 of anything but a post, raises body-not-replayable (Streams).

A redirect the rules do not follow raises &http-redirect-error: too-many-redirects, bad-location (a Location that is no URL, or names a scheme but http or https), insecure-redirect or body-not-replayable. http-redirect-error-response holds the response, and http-redirect-error-location its Location. Which Location is no URL varies by libcurl: 7.85.0 follows javascript:alert(1) as a relative path, where later ones refuse it, bad-location. Two Location headers vary too: 7.85.0, 8.5.0 and 8.7.1 follow the first, and 8.20.0 and 8.23.0 fail the transfer, 8.

Retries

Nothing is retried unless the session or the call names a policy, made by make-http-retry. A call's retry #f turns its session's off.

Key What Default
attempts transfers per step, the first among them 3
backoff-ms the first retry's wait, doubling for each later one 200
max-backoff-ms the longest wait, a Retry-After's too 10000
jitter #t waits a random share of the backoff; #f the backoff itself #t
statuses the statuses retried, 400 to 599 (429 503)
methods the methods a status, or a failure after a request was sent, is retried for (get head put delete)
  • A step is one URL. It makes the policy's attempts at most, and a redirect starts the count again.
  • A status is retried when the policy's statuses hold it and its methods the call's. A proxy that refused a CONNECT with a status counts as a response with that status.
  • A failure is retried when it is a connect, send-receive or timeout failure, but the call's own bound: for any method when nothing was sent, and otherwise only for one of methods. On libcurl 8.7.0 and 8.7.1 a transfer's size is unknown, so one that ran counts as sent: a post there is not retried after a refused connect.
  • The wait is the backoff, capped, a random share with jitter; or a retried status's Retry-After, seconds or a date. A wait longer than max-backoff-ms, or than the time the call has left, makes no retry.
  • A retried response joins no history. http-exchange-transfers, on what a call raises, counts every transfer.
  • A retry that runs out of time having sent nothing answers the last response it retried, or raises the last failure; one whose request may have gone out raises &http-timeout.
  • A producer's body is never retried once libcurl has asked for it. A refused connect, before any ask, is still retried.

libcurl resends on its own, with no policy: a request on a reused connection that died before any response byte, and an HTTP/2 stream the server refused with REFUSED_STREAM, up to five times more, a post's among them. A policy's attempts multiply them: a get retried 3 times can reach such a server 18 times.
