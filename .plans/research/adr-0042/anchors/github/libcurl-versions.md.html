What varies by libcurl

chez-curl hands most of a transfer to libcurl, so some of what a caller meets depends on which libcurl is loaded. This page lists each such behaviour, by topic. Everything here was measured on these libcurls, the ones the suites run:

Column The libcurl
7.85.0 curl 7.85.0 built from source with OpenSSL
8.5.0 Ubuntu 24.04's 8.5.0, with OpenSSL and with GnuTLS, alike unless a cell says
8.7.1 Apple's 8.7.1, on macOS, with CURL_SSL_BACKEND=secure-transport, which make test sets there
8.20.0 Guix's 8.20.0, with GnuTLS
8.23.0 curl 8.23.0 built from source with OpenSSL

A number in a cell is libcurl's code: the transfer fails with &curl-transfer-error, reason perform-failed, of the class Errors gives that code. "Alike" means every libcurl answered the same. A cell with two answers raced: either can come.

The tables are held by the suites, by version. Each section then lists what was seen and no test holds by version, and where libcurls differ but chez-curl answers alike on every one, which no test holds by version either: the tests hold chez-curl's answer.

Multipart

A file part is read as the body goes, after the engine's last check. A file changed after that check:

What 7.85.0 8.5.0 8.7.1 8.20.0 8.23.0
grown, HTTP/1.1 the extra bytes sent past Content-Length: the server's answer as 7.85.0 200, the body never closed as 8.7.1 as 8.7.1
grown, HTTP/2 92 the server's answer: 92 or 56 200, never closed as 8.7.1 as 8.7.1
shrunk, HTTP/1.1 waits until timeout-ms, 28, or for ever with none as 7.85.0 26, at once as 8.7.1 as 8.7.1
shrunk, HTTP/2 92, at once as 7.85.0 26 26 26
removed 26, alike
swapped for a FIFO held until a writer comes, then 200: alike
  • A resend, on a kept connection that died or an HTTP/2 stream refused at its head, sends the whole body again, alike.
  • A connection reset as a 4 MiB file part's request arrives is resent, or fails 55, by a race, alike.
  • The request size of a failed multipart transfer counts the head alone on 7.85.0 and 8.5.0, the head and the body sent on 8.20.0 and 8.23.0, and is #f on 8.7.1.
  • The default part types are alike on every libcurl: see The high level.

Where libcurls differ, and chez-curl answers alike; no test holds these by version:

  • A file part's path libcurl refuses, 26, is still sent, as an empty field on 8.20.0 and 8.23.0: chez-curl fails the transfer, part-file, on any answer but 0.
  • libcurl's escaping option sends CR and LF raw in a part's name on 7.85.0, 8.5.0, 8.7.1 and 8.20.0: chez-curl owns it, never sets it, and refuses those bytes.

  • 7.85.0 let a request ride a tunnel another user's credentials opened (CVE-2026-3784): before 8.19.0 chez-curl shares no tunnel.

Where it ends at your put, libcurl still asks for the rest of the body: a producer is fed it.

Seen, and no test holds it by version:

  • 8.5.0 over HTTP/2 can read nothing from a transfer paused for sending until the body resumes, rarely. A head wait then answers #f at its bound. Put or end the body first, or bound the wait and then put.
  • 7.85.0: an early HTTP/2 200 while a whole bytevector body is still owed never ends.
  • At the low level, an HTTP/2 3xx that comes before a body is sent whole never ends on 8.20.0 and 8.23.0, as the high level's row above.

Where libcurls differ, and chez-curl answers alike; no test holds these by version:

  • 7.85.0 and 8.5.0 lose a refusal of a piece made while a paused transfer resumes, and 8.7.1 hands a refused piece again: the reason chez-curl recorded wins on every libcurl.
  • Ubuntu's 8.5.0 spins the CPU while a transfer is paused, and 7.85.0 during a paused HTTP/2 upload: the engine paces its poll.
  • On 8.7.1 one HTTP/2 stream's failure fails its neighbours, and a paused one fills libcurl's 64 MiB cap: on 8.7.0 and 8.7.1 the engine does not multiplex.
  • An Expect: 100-continue anywhere in the value counts on 7.85.0 and 8.5.0, as a list element alone on 8.20.0 and 8.23.0: a stream refuses either.

Seen, and no test holds it by version:

  • On 7.85.0 a stalled lookup holds a group's wait for its calls, as it holds the engine's cancel, up to glibc's resolver timeout.

Responses and requests

Seen, and no test holds it by version:

  • On a reused connection 8.20.0 reported the name-lookup, connect and TLS-handshake timings as 0, and 7.85.0 a small connect time.
  • 8.5.0 hands a response header's empty value over as a lone CR, in curl-response-headers too.
  • Reading many headers costs about n² before 8.23.0. The cap's 1,000 lines bounds it.
  • A request URL past about 131,000 chars waited until its timeout on 8.5.0; 7.85.0, 8.20.0 and 8.23.0 sent ones of about a million.
