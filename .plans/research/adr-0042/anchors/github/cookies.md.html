Cookies

A cookie jar keeps the cookies servers set, and sends them back where they belong. Nothing keeps cookies unless you ask: no request sends or stores one without a jar.

The examples on this page call the test server: start it first, with node tests/origin.mjs, and give an example the address after "http": in the line it prints, not raw's or h2c's (The examples).

At the high level

A session made with cookies #t keeps a jar on its engine. Every call of the session carries it, and every request a redirect makes, to another origin too: libcurl matches cookies to hosts. The session's close frees it.

(call-with-http-session 'cookies #t
  (lambda (session)
    (http-get session "https://example.com/login")
    (http-session-cookies session)))     ; its cookies, as lines
  • http-session-cookies reads the jar out, and http-session-load-cookies! loads lines into it, as the low level's read-out and load do (below).
  • A call takes no cookies key: the jar is the session's.
  • A Cookie or Host header, the session's or a call's, is refused beside it, cookie-beside-jar.
  • On a session made without cookies, both procedures are an assertion violation.

At the low level

A jar is an engine's. Give it to each request whose cookies it should keep:

(define engine (make-curl-engine))
(define jar (make-curl-cookie-jar engine))

;; fetch : String -> Response
(define (fetch path)
  (curl-token-await
   (curl-submit engine (make-curl-request (string-append base path)
                                          'cookie-jar jar 'timeout-ms 10000))))

Read it out, load lines into it, and close it:

;; A load takes lines in the form a read-out gives them.
(curl-cookie-jar-load! jar '("127.0.0.1\tFALSE\t/\tFALSE\t0\tsize\tlarge"))
(for-each (lambda (line) (write line) (newline))
          (list-sort string<? (curl-cookie-jar-cookies jar)))

(curl-cookie-jar-close! jar)
(curl-engine-stop engine)
  • The rules are libcurl's: which cookies are kept, and which go to which host and path. They vary by version.
  • A jar holds cookies alone, in memory: no file, and no HSTS entry.
  • A line is a Netscape line, DOMAIN TAILMATCH PATH SECURE EXPIRY NAME VALUE, tab-separated, #HttpOnly_ before an HttpOnly cookie's domain. It is a Latin-1 string both ways: each byte one char. Their order moves: compare them sorted.
  • A close frees the jar once no transfer holds it, and an engine's stop frees every jar. A second close does nothing.
  • Each jar procedure waits for the engine, with no bound, as a stop does, and none may run inside a critical section. A stopped or failed engine's jar raises as a submit to it would.
  • A load is slow for many lines, about n², and runs on the engine's thread, so its other transfers wait for it.
  • A libcurl that refuses a jar's make, read-out or load raises Chez's &error, no chez-curl condition.

What a load takes

A load takes a line in the form a read-out writes it, and checks every line before it loads any. A line needs:

  • seven fields, none holding a control char, U+0000 to U+001F or U+007F;
  • TRUE or FALSE in the second and the fourth, the domain starting with a dot exactly beside TRUE in the second;
  • a domain, after any #HttpOnly_, neither empty nor starting with #, and no Set-Cookie: line;
  • a path starting with /;
  • an expiry of decimal digits, at most 9223372036854775807;
  • a name; no ; in the name or the value, and no = in the name;
  • a name and value of 4,096 bytes at most together;
  • a name starting __Secure- or __Host-, in any case, only beside TRUE in the fourth, and for __Host- the path / and FALSE in the second;
  • no char above U+00FF.

Any other is refused, cookie-line, naming the line's index, from 0, never its text: a cookie's value is a secret. Nothing is loaded then.

A read-out can give lines a load refuses, by version: see What varies by libcurl.

Refusals

  • When you make the request: a Cookie or Host header beside a jar, in any case, any value, cookie-beside-jar: libcurl would send a second Cookie, or match the jar against your Host.
  • When you submit: another engine's jar, cookie-jar-engine, and a closed one, cookie-jar-closed. A close that crosses a submit fails its token so.
  • A long head on 7.85.0 can leave a request unended until its timeout: its cap on the Cookie line counts all the head written before it, the request line, Host, Authorization and User-Agent among it. Pass a timeout-ms (What varies).
