Groups: many calls at once

One engine thread runs many requests. A session's engine is one thread, and it drives every transfer submitted to it at once. So to make many requests at once you need no thread per request: start them all from your one thread, and wait for them.

At the high level, a request group does it, and http-parallel does it for a list. At the low level, submit many requests to one engine and await their tokens, or take them from a mailbox (The low level).

The examples on this page call the test server: start it first, with node tests/origin.mjs, and give an example the address after "http": in the line it prints, not raw's or h2c's (The examples).

    ;; http-parallel: a list of calls, at most two at once; the responses
    ;; come back in the list's order, whichever ends first.
    (write (map http-response-status
                (http-parallel session
                               (list (list 'get (url "/status/200"))
                                     (list 'get (url "/status/201"))
                                     (list 'get (url "/status/404")))
                               'limit 2)))
    (newline)
    ;; A group: go starts a call and hands back its pending at once;
    ;; http-await gives its response.
    (call-with-request-group session
      (lambda (go)
        (let* ((a (go 'get (url "/status/202")))
               (b (go 'get (url "/status/203"))))
          (write (list (http-response-status (http-await a))
                       (http-response-status (http-await b))))
          (newline))))))
(200 201 404)
(202 203)

A request group

(call-with-request-group SESSION KEY VALUE ... PROC) calls PROC with go, on your thread.

  • (go METHOD URL KEY VALUE ...) starts a call, as http-request takes one, and answers its pending at once. Its method, keys and URL are checked first: a refusal raises from go, and starts nothing.
  • http-await gives a pending's response, or raises its failure, the same any number of times. http-pending-done? moves the group on one turn and tells whether the call has ended. http-pending-cancel ends it as cancelled.
  • Whichever call your thread waits for, it drives them all, so one call's redirect or retry goes on while you await another.
  • limit caps how many calls run at once. The rest wait their turn, first in, first out, each on its own clock from its go: a bound that passes while it waits fails it with &http-timeout. A call keeps its slot through every redirect, retry and wait.
  • The group waits for every call before it returns, and then raises the first failure you never awaited. A call you cancelled raises nothing.
  • If PROC escapes, the group cancels every call, waits for each transfer to end, and lets the escape go on. A stalled name lookup can hold that wait on some libcurls (What varies). A second Ctrl-C during it gives up, and leaves those transfers running.
  • Only the thread that made a group may use it: another raises &http-group-error, wrong-thread. A go once PROC has returned raises closed, and the await of a cancelled call raises cancelled.
  • A group takes no producer: go refuses one, stream-in-group.
  • Inside a Chez engine, an expiry ends the whole group: once resumed, its awaits raise cancelled, and a new go raises closed.
  • None of it may run inside a critical section.

http-parallel

(http-parallel SESSION SPECS KEY VALUE ...) runs each spec, (METHOD URL KEY VALUE ...), in one group, its one key limit, and answers the responses in the specs' order.

  • It checks every call before it sends any, as a submit would check it.
  • The first call to fail raises, as it raised, once every other is cancelled and its transfer has ended.

What can still fail partway

Once calls run, each is its own: what becomes of one is its answer alone, never raised by go. A submit can still refuse a call after its checks passed: a stopped or failed engine; a file part no longer a regular file, part-file; or a redirect's next request, or a retry, refused as any request would be, prior-knowledge-over-https, credentials-beside-userinfo or proxy-tls-in-tls (Redirects and retries).
