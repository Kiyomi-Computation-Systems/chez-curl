The high level: (http-client)

A session holds one engine, and the defaults its calls share. A call takes its session first, blocks its thread until the response, and answers it whatever the status: a status is data.

The examples on this page call the test server: start it first, with node tests/origin.mjs, and give an example the address after "http": in the line it prints, not raw's or h2c's (The examples).

(call-with-http-session
 (lambda (session)
   (let ((response (http-get session (string-append base "/status/200"))))
     (display (http-response-status response))
     (newline)
     (display (http-response-text response)))))

Sessions

  • call-with-http-session KEY VALUE ... PROC makes a session, calls PROC with it, and closes it when PROC returns or escapes.
  • make-http-session KEY VALUE ... makes one that lives until http-session-close. The close stops its engine: each call in flight ends as stopped, and a later call raises &curl-engine-error. It does not wait for calls to finish. A second close returns.
  • There is no default session, and nothing starts a thread you did not ask for: the session's engine is its one thread. Threads may share a session.
  • Its keys are checked before its engine is made, so a refusal leaves none.
  • A second Ctrl-C, or a timer, that interrupts the close itself can leave the engine running until the process exits. chez-curl does not prevent this.

A session takes the low level's keys, each a default for its calls: headers, user-agent, timeout-ms, connect-timeout-ms, body-limit, ca-file, verify, proxy, proxy-ca-file, proxy-verify, http-version and credentials (Reference has each), and four of its own:

Key What Default
redirects the most redirects a call follows; #f follows none 20
allow-downgrade follow a redirect from https to http #f
retry a policy from make-http-retry, or #f #f
cookies keep a cookie jar (Cookies) #f

A call's value replaces its session's. A call's header replaces every session header of its name, in any case; a call's User-Agent header sets no user-agent, and a call's user-agent drops the session's User-Agent header (Headers and JSON). Redirects and retries has the first three.

Verbs and responses

(http-request SESSION METHOD URL KEY VALUE ...) makes a call: METHOD one of get, head, post, put, patch and delete, URL an http:// or https:// URL. http-get, http-head, http-post, http-put, http-patch and http-delete name the method for you.

A response holds:

  • http-response-status, an integer, and http-response-http-version;
  • http-response-url, the URL its transfer asked for: the call's, its params appended, or a redirect's;
  • http-response-headers, a list of (NAME . VALUE) Latin-1 strings, and http-response-header, the first of a name, in any case, or #f;
  • http-response-body, a bytevector, and http-response-text, the body as UTF-8: U+FFFD for a malformed sequence, a leading byte-order mark dropped, no charset read;
  • http-response-timings, each phase's microseconds from the start;
  • http-response-history, the redirects it followed, oldest first.

Bodies, forms and params

  • body is a bytevector, sent as it is; a string, sent as UTF-8, with no Content-Type of its own; or a producer, which streams.
  • form is a list of (NAME . VALUE) strings, sent as the body, encoded as application/x-www-form-urlencoded, with that Content-Type unless your headers name one.
  • params is a list of the same, encoded the same way and appended to the URL's query.
  • No body waits for a 100. libcurl adds Expect: 100-continue to a body past 1 MiB, and chez-curl takes it away; an Expect header of yours is sent instead.
    ;; params are encoded and appended to the URL's query.
    (let ((echo (lines (http-response-text
                        (http-get session (string-append base "/echo")
                                  'params '(("q" . "chez scheme") ("page" . "2")))))))
      (display (car echo))
      (newline))
    ;; form is sent as application/x-www-form-urlencoded.
    (let ((echo (lines (http-response-text
                        (http-post session (string-append base "/echo")
                                   'form '(("name" . "Ada Lovelace") ("lang" . "Scheme")))))))
GET /echo?q=chez+scheme&page=2 HTTP/1.1
POST /echo HTTP/1.1
Content-Type: application/x-www-form-urlencoded
name=Ada+Lovelace&lang=Scheme

A get or head refuses a body, a form and a multipart body, body-not-allowed, and body and form together are refused, body-and-form.

Multipart

multipart sends a multipart/form-data body, a list of parts. libcurl frames it, and writes its Content-Type and boundary.

           (http-post session (string-append base "/parts?key=example-multipart")
                      'multipart
                      '(("title" "Holiday")
                        ("notes" #vu8(104 105) filename "notes.txt")
                        ("doc" (file "examples/data/hello.txt"))
                        ("blob" #vu8(1 2 3) type "application/x-thing")))))
  • A part is (NAME SOURCE OPTION ...): SOURCE a string, sent as UTF-8, a bytevector, or (file PATH); each OPTION filename STRING or type STRING, at most once. A file part's filename is its path's last component unless you give one. No part streams, nests parts, is encoded, or takes headers of its own.
  • Its strings are checked: a name, filename or type holding a control char, U+0000 to U+001F or U+007F, or a name or filename holding ", is refused, part-bytes; an empty one, part-empty; no part at all, multipart-empty. A refusal names the part, (part 2 filename), never the string.
  • A part's type is the type you give. Else libcurl picks one by the filename's last extension, in any case: .gif, .jpg, .jpeg, .png, .svg, .txt, .htm, .html, .pdf and .xml alone. A file part with none of those takes its path's, else application/octet-stream; a data part gets no Content-Type. Alike on every libcurl.
  • A Content-Type header beside it is refused, multipart-content-type; a session's is dropped. Beside body or form it is refused, multipart-beside-body, and get and head refuse it.
  • It is a whole body, sent once per request: a retry resends it, a 307 or 308 keeps it, a 303 drops it, and a group takes one.

Files. A relative path is resolved against the current directory once, as the call starts. A data part's bytes are copied then, so a retry or a redirect sends what the call began with. A file part is read again by each transfer, so a retry or a redirect sends what the file holds by then. Each path must name a regular file, symbolic links followed, as the call starts, at each submit, and as the engine starts the transfer, else part-file. libcurl reads the file as the body goes, so:

  • A file that changes after that check is yours. What libcurl then sends, grown, shrunk or removed, varies by libcurl.
  • A file whose size is not its content, in /proc or /sys, was seen to send what a shrunk one sends; no test holds it.
  • A path swapped for a FIFO after the check holds the engine's thread until a writer comes, perhaps for ever: every transfer on that engine stalls, and its stop, a session's close among them, never returns.

The checks narrow that window, and cannot close it. If you cannot trust the file system, read the file yourself and send its bytes as a bytevector part.

A libcurl built without multipart support refuses it when you submit, multipart-unsupported. The low level takes the same parts as a request's body. Its relative paths are resolved as the request is made, a data part's bytes are copied at each submit, and a file part is read by each transfer:

(make-curl-request url 'method 'post
                   'body '(multipart ("title" "Holiday") ("doc" (file "/tmp/a.txt"))))

Timeouts and limits

  • timeout-ms bounds the whole call, from its start: each transfer, a redirect's and a retry's among them, gets the time left. At the bound the call raises &http-timeout, with (curl)'s &curl-timeout. #f, the default, is no bound.
  • connect-timeout-ms bounds each connection, its TLS handshake among it.
  • body-limit bounds the response body's bytes: past it the call raises &curl-too-large (Errors).
  • Every transfer caps its response headers at 1,000 lines or 102,400 bytes, counted over the whole transfer: every head, a 1xx, a proxy's CONNECT answer and each auth round's among them, and the trailers. Past either it raises &curl-too-large, reason header-limit. No key sets the cap.

A call's checks pause Ctrl-C and garbage collection. While a call checks what you passed, a Ctrl-C waits, and so does every other thread's garbage collection. For an ordinary call that is brief, but it grows with what you pass: a large body, a long form, many headers or params, a long URL. So bound what you take from untrusted input before you pass it. A URL past libcurl's cap of 8,000,000 bytes is refused at once, malformed-input.

A call that is escaped cancels its transfer: a Ctrl-C whose handler escapes, a timer's, a continuation, or a Chez engine's expiry. A call made inside a critical section is refused before it sends anything: its wait could not end there.
