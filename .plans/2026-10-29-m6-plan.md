- **Status:** draft, revised on 2026-10-29 after an adversarial review of the plan, again after
  a review of those revisions (§10), and with the Mac's run on its PR (§2.4), before it merged. A

M6 streams bodies both ways, with backpressure (brief §2 item 7, §5). After M6 a caller can:

- read a response's body as it arrives, a piece at a time or through a binary input port, and
  stop reading to stop the transfer: over HTTP/1.1 the server's writes then stall in the kernel's
  buffers (§2);
- send a body as a producer gives it, of a length given or not;
- do both through the high level: `call-with-response-port`, and a producer as a call's `body`
  (ADR-0031).

- **The low level keeps its stand,** no policy (brief §6.1). A streamed body is a request's key,
  and the token's operations (ADR-0030); the engine gains its fourth message, resume, and paces a
  poll that spins (ADR-0012, amended 2026-10-29).
- **Caller code never runs on the engine's thread** (ADR-0012, decision 4). The engine's
  callables copy between libcurl's buffers and bounded buffers, and signal. A consumer reads, and
  a producer is called, on the caller's thread.
- **A streamed body is sent at most once** (ADR-0032).
- **HTTP/2 multiplexing's memory, cookies, auth and a proxy given by URL are M7's** (brief §8;
  ADR-0030, decision 5).
- **M6 has three slices:** the response stream and the engine's resume; the request stream; the
  high level.

## 2. Measured before planning (2026-10-29)

### 2.1 Pause and resume, through C

[`research/m6/pause/`](research/m6/pause/): a C probe on the multi interface whose loop steps as
the engine does: perform, info read, a poll of at most 1000 ms, and a resume step that calls
`curl_easy_pause` outside any callback. It ran against a server of its own, with plaintext, TLS
and HTTP/2 faces, the transfers set as the engine sets them. It ran on 7.85.0, Ubuntu's 8.5.0
with OpenSSL and with GnuTLS, Guix's 8.20.0 in the container, and 8.23.0, two runs each, in two
rounds: 12 cases, then 18 more. Every probe exited 0 and no setopt was refused. libcurl's live
bytes are counted through `curl_global_init_mem`, so they leave out nghttp2's and the TLS
library's.

**Downloads:**

- **A refused chunk comes back whole, inside `curl_easy_pause`, every byte in order,** on all
  five. A replay can be longer than the chunk refused, starting with its bytes: over chunked
  HTTP/1.1 on all five, and over HTTP/2 on 7.85.0, 8.20.0 and 8.23.0. No call ever held more than
  16,384 bytes, at any `BUFFERSIZE`, 524,288 among them.
- **Over HTTP/1.1 a paused transfer stops reading its socket.** libcurl's live bytes did not
  grow during a 2 s hold, 36,555 to 153,862 bytes over every face, and the server's writes
  stalled after 2,637,824 to 2,772,992 bytes, plaintext and TLS, for the hold's 2 s.
- **Over HTTP/2, a paused stream alone on its connection** held 75 to 154 KB of libcurl's.
  **Beside an active stream on the same connection,** it kept receiving: in 5 s libcurl's live
  bytes reached 66.9 MB on 7.85.0, 10.6 MB on 8.5.0, 21.1 MB on 8.20.0 and 33.5 MB on 8.23.0, as
  the server's writes stopped at the stream's window, 33,619,968, 10,551,296, 20,971,520 and
  20,971,520 bytes. The other stream was never stalled. No lane reached libcurl's 64 MiB cap
  (ADR-0030, decision 5).
- **`TIMEOUT_MS` runs while paused:** 28 at 999 to 1,007 ms for a bound of 1,000, on every face
  (ADR-0031, decision 3).
- **Ubuntu's 8.5.0 makes the poll spin while a transfer is paused:** 6.2 to 7.0 million polls in
  a 3 s pause, each under 1 ms, at 100% of a core, over every face; the other three polled 3 to
  5 times (ADR-0012, amended 2026-10-29).
- **A refusal inside the replay is lost on 7.85.0 and 8.5.0:** `curl_easy_pause` answers 23,
  and the transfer ends 0 without the chunk it refused, 16,307 to 16,384 bytes; 8.20.0 and 8.23.0
  end it with 23.
- **`curl_easy_pause(CONT)`** answers 0 for a transfer not paused, and 43 for one done, removed
  or never added.
- **The head ends with an empty line in the header callback, each 1xx head too,** and the first
  write callback comes after the final head's empty line: for a body sent 300 ms after its head,
  the empty line came 0.3 to 46.4 ms into the transfer and the first write 301.2 to 307.9 ms, so
  the head is known before the body. At a 1xx head's empty line, `CURLINFO_RESPONSE_CODE` is 100
  or 103 and `curl_easy_nextheader` gives no `CURLH_HEADER` header; at the final one it gives the
  final head. A 204 makes no write call.
- **A cancel while paused, `curl_multi_remove_handle`,** answers 0 and frees libcurl's buffers;
  the next transfer reused the connection over HTTP/2 alone.
- **The low-speed check:** paused 3 s with `LOW_SPEED_LIMIT` 1000 and `LOW_SPEED_TIME` 1, every
  transfer ended 0. Its control, a server that stalls 3 s, unpaused, gave 28 over HTTP/1.1 on
  all five, plaintext and TLS: at 1,205 to 2,212 ms on 7.85.0, at 1,048 ms over TLS on 8.23.0,
  and otherwise only as the stall ended, 3,013 to 3,019 ms. Over HTTP/2 it gave 28 on 7.85.0
  and 8.20.0, on 8.5.0 with OpenSSL in one run of two, and none on 8.5.0 with GnuTLS nor on
  8.23.0. No idle bound is built on it here (§9).

**Uploads,** a read callback's body:

- **The framing, 240 transfers a run:** a length given sends `Content-Length`; -1 sends
  `Transfer-Encoding: chunked` over HTTP/1.1 and neither over HTTP/2. `POST`, `PUT` by `UPLOAD`,
  and `PATCH` by `CUSTOMREQUEST` beside either, sent their method and 262,144 bytes whole, by
  SHA-256. libcurl sends `Expect: 100-continue` for a size of -1, and on 7.85.0 for a `PUT` of
  any size, never over HTTP/2 nor beside an empty `Expect:`.
- **`CURL_READFUNC_PAUSE` resumes after `curl_easy_pause(CONT)`,** at the next perform, never
  inside the call, the body whole and in order, on every face, chunked and sized.
- **A body short of its length:** 28 at the bound over HTTP/1.1 on 7.85.0 and 8.5.0, which
  stall, 92 over HTTP/2; 26 at once on 8.20.0 and 8.23.0 (ADR-0031, decision 2).
- **An overrun, 1,500 bytes into a `Content-Length` of 1,000:** over HTTP/1.1, 7.85.0 and 8.5.0
  send the surplus and keep the connection, where the server read 500 bytes it could not parse;
  over HTTP/2 they fail, 92 or 56. 8.20.0 and 8.23.0 offer 1,000 bytes at most.
- **A size of 0** never calls the read callback, and sends `Content-Length: 0`.
- **libcurl's own resend:** a pooled connection that died before any response byte makes
  libcurl rewind and send again. Without a `SEEKFUNCTION`, or with one that answers `CANTSEEK`,
  the rewind fails with 65, on all five, before the next attempt: `PREREQFUNCTION` ran once.
  On 8.23.0 it meets even a connection the server closed cleanly 200 ms before. An HTTP/2
  `REFUSED_STREAM` after 32 KiB of DATA: 65, or 56 on 7.85.0, the prerequest callable run once.
  `FRESH_CONNECT` ended 120 runs of 120 at 0, with one request (ADR-0032, amended).
- **The low-speed check while the send is paused** gave no 28 on any of the five in 5 s.
- **An answer while the read is paused** (413 or a full 200, with a close or keep-alive, and over
  HTTP/2): the status always arrived; whether the transfer then ended without a resume differed:
  on none of the five cases on 7.85.0, on the closes and HTTP/2's 200 on 8.5.0, on every HTTP/1.1
  case and HTTP/2's 413 on 8.20.0 and 8.23.0 (§3.6, §5.1).

### 2.2 Read in libcurl's source, not measured

A source reading of the five trees, 8.5.0's upstream and Apple's 8.7.1 unpatched, with curl's
changelog and commits from 7.86.0 to 8.23.0, by an Opus agent and three of its own, its key
citations checked again ([`research/m6/source-reading.md`](research/m6/source-reading.md); the
8.5.0 tarball's hash and each cited commit's first release, in
[`research/m6/source-provenance.txt`](research/m6/source-provenance.txt)). Among its findings,
those the probe did not measure:

- **8.7.1 may resend without rewinding:** its follow step drops the result of the reset that
  rewinds a body (`curl-8.7.1/lib/transfer.c:915`), so a resend could read on from mid-stream.
  The prerequest callable closes it on every version (ADR-0032, amended). The Mac confirmed it
  (§2.4).
- **8.7.1 keeps a paused HTTP/2 stream's window open,** so beside an active stream it buffers
  until libcurl's 64 MiB cap fails it with 100 (`curl-8.7.1/lib/cw-out.c:318-319`). The Mac
  confirmed it, and worse: the connection failed with it (§2.4).
- **8.7.1 writes the final head's empty line before it analyses the head** (#13752, fixed in
  8.9.0), so `CURLINFO_CONTENT_LENGTH_DOWNLOAD_T` reads -1 there. The head reads no length. The
  Mac confirmed it (§2.4).
- **`CURLPAUSE_SEND_CONT` and `CURLPAUSE_CONT` are both 0:** the call takes the whole new state
  (`curl-7.85.0/include/curl/curl.h:3092-3099`).
- **`curl_easy_pause` never blocks and never calls the read callback;** it calls the write and
  header callbacks, and on 8.20.0 and 8.23.0 the content decoders, before it returns.
- **A proxy's CONNECT head** reads `CURLINFO_RESPONSE_CODE` 0 at its empty line: libcurl clears
  the code there, on 7.85.0, 8.20.0 and 8.23.0 (§3.2). Slice 1 measured it 0 on all five, 8.5.0
  too (§4.1).
- **Hazards no probe reached:** 7.85.0 does not end a paused upload when the connection is lost
  (#11769, fixed in 8.3.0); 8.5.0 stalls an HTTP/2 transfer with one direction paused
  (cdd905a9, fixed in 8.6.0); 8.5.0 and 8.7.1 spin during an HTTP/2 upload (#16084, fixed in
  8.12.0). Slice 2 measured each (§5.1, as measured): 7.85.0 missed a FIN under a paused
  upload; 8.5.0's stall was real with OpenSSL; the HTTP/2 upload's spin was 7.85.0's, not
  8.5.0's.
- **8.23.0's unchecked reuse of a connection pooled under 1 s before came with 8.22.0**
  (2f1e94ea) **and is fixed after 8.23.0** (ef6b17cf, 2026-10-15, in no release yet): the curl
  bug report M5 carried is moot (§9).

### 2.3 Chez's custom ports

[`research/m6/ports/`](research/m6/ports/): ten cases on Chez 10.4.0 in the container, two runs
each; 10.4.1's port code is the same source.

- **`read!` may answer fewer bytes than asked; 0 means the end, and the end is not sticky:**
  `get-u8`, `get-bytevector-some` and `-some!` call `read!` again after a 0.
- **A bad answer** raises `&assertion`, non-continuable, and the port works after.
- **Chez holds no lock and opens no critical section around `read!`:** inside
  `critical-section` another thread's `(collect)` raises, and `collect-rendezvous` waited out
  the read's whole bound.
- **A `close-port` from another thread races a read:** a reader that touched the eof flag lost
  the closed bit in 1,019 and 898 trials of 2,000; a control that did not, in none.
- **A raise from `read!`** loses bytes earlier `read!` calls gave inside one `get-bytevector-n`,
  `-n!` or `-all`; `get-bytevector-some`, `-some!` and `get-u8` lose none of those.
- **A timer's expiry swept a tick at a time across `read!`'s return lost no byte** on any path,
  251 ticks each; a planted `read!` that loops after its take lost 5 bytes, the control. A timer
  cannot land at a critical section's end, though, and **a keyboard interrupt can**: the review's
  probe ([`research/m6/review/`](research/m6/review/)) blocked a `read!`'s take on its mutex,
  sent SIGINT, and a handler that escaped as the take's section ended lost the piece taken, under
  `get-bytevector-some` and `get-u8` alike, 3 runs of 3, two the review's and one mine; a handler
  that returned lost none (§3.5).
- **No port is closed by a collection or at exit.** `file-buffer-size` is 8,192, the count
  `get-bytevector-some` asks for; `custom-port-buffer-size` is 128, read when the port is made.

### 2.4 Apple's 8.7.1, on the Mac (2026-10-29, on the plan's PR)

The maintainer ran both rounds' 30 cases against Apple's 8.7.1, SecureTransport in use, under
Node 20.10.0, and the reset that splits
([`mac-8.7.1.log`](research/m6/pause/results/mac-8.7.1.log),
[`mac-8.7.1-split.log`](research/m6/pause/results/mac-8.7.1-split.log)). Every case exited 0. Its
answers fill §3.7's 8.7.1 column, and the four readings of §2.2 the Mac was to measure:

- **A resend without a rewind, confirmed:** the failed rewind, 65, is logged and ignored, and
  libcurl sends the request again, reading on from mid-stream. A reset after 81,920 bytes of an 8
  MiB body brought a second request carrying the body's last 7,389,184 bytes, which ended 26 when
  the body ran out; chunked, the server would have taken a cut body as whole. An HTTP/2 refused
  stream, with no seek, reached the server twice and ended 26. A pooled connection that died
  under a streamed body ended 26 with no seek or with one that answers `CANTSEEK`, where the
  Linux libcurls end 65. In each, the prerequest callable ran for the second attempt,
  `prereq_calls=2`, before its head was sent: on 8.7.1 it is the guard that holds (ADR-0032,
  amended).
- **The HTTP/2 window, worse than read:** a paused stream beside an active one filled libcurl's
  64 MiB cap, 67,187,470 live bytes, and failed the connection: the active stream ended 56
  after 234 ms. And a write callback's error on one stream failed its sibling with 16 (#13292).
  The engine turns multiplexing off on 8.7.0 and 8.7.1 (ADR-0030, amended; §3.4).
- **The head's length, confirmed:** `CURLINFO_CONTENT_LENGTH_DOWNLOAD_T` read -1 at the final
  head's empty line, over every face.
- **The spin, not there:** 3 to 6 polls in a 3 s pause.

The rest answered as the Linux libcurls did, or as a row of §3.7 says: a refused chunk comes back
whole inside the unpause; `TIMEOUT_MS` ends a paused transfer with 28 at 1,006 to 1,007 ms; every
head ends with its own empty line; `FRESH_CONNECT` ended 12 runs of 12 at 0 with one request; and
the low-speed check never fired, not even against a server that stalls, unpaused, over any face.

### 2.5 So

- **The premise holds on every Linux libcurl:** pause and resume work both ways, and backpressure
  reaches the server over HTTP/1.1.
- **The engine must own what libcurl does not do alike:** a pause's failure, a body's length, a
  resend, and a poll that spins.
- **What stays libcurl's is documented:** HTTP/2's memory beside an active stream, and the
  timeout's running while paused (§3.7).

## 3. Decisions that bind every slice

### 3.1 The public surface

- **`make-curl-request` gains three keys** (ADR-0030):
  - `stream-buffer`: a positive exact integer, the bytes a token buffers of the response's body;
    or `#f`, the default, which keeps the whole body, as before;
  - `body` may also be the symbol `stream`, with `post`, `put`, `patch` and `delete`, as a whole
    body is (ADR-0023);
  - `body-length`: an exact natural below 2^63 - 1, the streamed body's length, sent as
    `Content-Length`; or `#f`, the default, which sends it chunked over HTTP/1.1 and as DATA
    frames over HTTP/2. With a body that does not stream it is refused, `&curl-request-error`,
    `body-length-without-stream`.

  With their accessors, `curl-request-stream-buffer` and `curl-request-body-length`.
  `prepare-transfer`'s plan and `sends-body?` take a streamed body: a streamed `delete` sends one,
  with `Content-Type:` and `Expect:`, as a `delete` with a whole body does.
- **The token gains** (ADR-0030, with the names the maintainer saw):
  - `curl-token-head-wait : Token Natural -> Boolean`: whether the head has arrived, or the
    token is done, within MS milliseconds;
  - `curl-token-head : Token -> (U Head #f)`: the final head once it has arrived; never waits;
  - `curl-head?`, `curl-head-status`, `curl-head-http-version` and `curl-head-headers`, as the
    response's accessors read them;
  - `curl-head-body-asked? : Head -> Boolean`: whether the request streams its body and libcurl
    had asked for it by that head (§3.2), which slice 3's at-most-once rule reads (ADR-0030,
    amended 2026-10-31; slice 2 builds it);
  - `curl-token-next-piece : Token Natural -> (U Bytevector Eof #f)`: the next piece, a
    bytevector of a byte or more that the caller owns; the eof object once every piece has been
    taken and the transfer has ended, however it ended, which `curl-token-outcome` then tells; or
    `#f` once MS milliseconds pass;
  - `curl-token-body-port : Token -> Binary-Input-Port`: a custom binary input port over the
    pieces (§3.5), once per token;
  - `curl-token-room-wait : Token Natural -> (U #t #f 'done)`: `#t` once libcurl has asked for
    the body and the request's buffer has room; `done` once the token is done; `#f` once MS pass;
  - `curl-token-put-piece! : Token Bytevector [Natural] -> (U #t #f 'done)`: the piece copied
    into the request's buffer, waiting while it is full, at most MS milliseconds when given:
    `#t` once taken, `#f` once MS pass, `done` once the token is done, the piece not taken;
  - `curl-token-end-body! : Token -> Void`: the body's end; on a token done, or a second time,
    nothing;
  - `curl-token-body-count : Token -> Natural`: the bytes the puts have committed (§3.5).

  A stream operation on a token whose request does not stream that way is an assertion
  violation.
- **`&curl-transfer-error` gains three reasons:** `pause-failed`, `curl_easy_pause` answered
  other than 0, with its code; `body-length-mismatch`, a streamed body short of its length, or a
  piece past it; `body-not-replayable`, a resend libcurl wanted once a byte was handed it
  (ADR-0032). None is classed (ADR-0022): each is chez-curl's. As `finish-transfer` does for
  `body-limit`, a reason a callable recorded wins over libcurl's code, `pause-failed`'s too.
- **`(http-client)` gains** (ADR-0031):
  - `call-with-response-port : Session Symbol String Any ... (Response Binary-Input-Port -> Any
    ...) -> Any ...`, whose own key, beside the call's, is `stream-buffer`, 65,536 by default;
  - `body` may be a producer, `( -> (U Bytevector Eof))`, and `body-length` gives its length;
  - `&http-redirect-error` gains the reason `body-not-replayable` (ADR-0032), and
    `&http-request-error` the reason `stream-in-group`.
- **The response `call-with-response-port` hands PROC** has a body of `#f` and timings of `#f`:
  the transfer has not ended. `http-response-text` refuses it, an assertion violation. Its history
  is whole.
- **Every export carries a Typed Racket style signature** (AGENTS.md).

### 3.2 The response stream

- **The write callable fills a bounded buffer.** It takes a chunk when the buffer holds less than
  `stream-buffer` bytes with the chunk added, or when the buffer is empty, so a buffer smaller
  than a chunk cannot stall; otherwise it marks the transfer paused for receiving and answers
  `CURL_WRITEFUNC_PAUSE`, the check and the mark in one hold of the stream's mutex. A token
  holds at most `stream-buffer` bytes or one chunk, 16,384 bytes at most (§2.1), whichever is
  more, and the piece a reader has in hand.
- **Each chunk is copied once,** by the checked copy, into a fresh bytevector, which becomes the
  piece a caller takes. The port keeps an offset into a piece it takes part of.
- **The head** is read by a header callable, set on a streamed response alone. It reads nothing
  until a line that is empty, `"\r\n"` or `"\n"`; then `CURLINFO_RESPONSE_CODE`, and below 200,
  a 1xx head's, or 0, a proxy's CONNECT head's (§2.2), it waits for the next head. At the first
  final head it reads the status, the version and every `CURLH_HEADER` header for request -1, as
  the response reads them, and whether libcurl had asked for the request's body by then, and
  publishes the head on the token, in one critical section. It reads no length (§2.2).
- **One transfer, one request:** libcurl makes a second request inside one transfer for an auth
  round or a followed redirect, and request -1 then moves on, so the first final head would be
  the 401's or the 3xx's. A request that streams its response refuses the private `follow`
  field, and the hatch's `HTTPAUTH` and `PROXYAUTH`, as `&curl-request-error`,
  `stream-with-rounds`; M7 revisits auth.
  - **And a 417:** a caller that names its own `Expect: 100-continue`, which replaces libcurl's
    empty `Expect:` (ADR-0023), makes libcurl wait for a 100, and on a 417 resend the request
    inside the same transfer. Slice 1's review measured the head published as the 417's, and
    the pieces and the outcome as the resend's 200, on 7.85.0, 8.5.0 and 8.20.0
    ([`research/m6/slice1-review/`](research/m6/slice1-review/)). Which values make libcurl wait
    moves with the version: 7.85.0 and 8.5.0 wait on `100-continue` anywhere in the value, in
    any ASCII case, `x100-continue` and `100-continue;q=1` among them; 8.20.0 and 8.23.0 only on
    an element of a comma list ([`research/m6/slice1-fix/expect/`](research/m6/slice1-fix/expect/)).
    So a request that streams its response refuses any `Expect` header of the caller's whose
    value holds `100-continue` anywhere, folded A to Z alone, as `stream-with-rounds`, by every
    path the others are refused by, and again at submit, on the submit's copies. Slice 3's
    `call-with-response-port` streams every response, so it meets the refusal too.
- **Every look at the buffer decides the resume** (§3.5): with the transfer paused for receiving,
  no resume queued, and the buffer at half of `stream-buffer` or less, it queues one, which the
  engine carries out (§3.4).
- **The stream ends when the transfer does:** the pieces buffered stay readable, whatever the
  outcome; after the last, `next-piece` answers the eof object, and the token's outcome is a
  response whose body is `#f`, or the failure. A transfer cannot end while paused with bytes
  held (§2.2), so its end follows the consumer. Nothing is dropped from the stream under the
  engine's mutex.
- **The body limit counts a stream's bytes** as a whole body's, by the same refusal.
- **The chunk log,** write-body's data for the thread test, keeps each chunk for a whole body;
  for a stream it keeps the count of each verdict and the threads it ran on, so a long stream's
  log stays small.

### 3.3 The request stream

- **The options, by method, as measured (§2.1):** `post`, `POST` with `READFUNCTION` and
  `POSTFIELDSIZE_LARGE`; `put`, `UPLOAD` with `INFILESIZE_LARGE`; `patch` and `delete`, `POST`
  with `CUSTOMREQUEST` and `POSTFIELDSIZE_LARGE`. The length is `body-length`, or -1. Beside them,
  `Content-Type:` and `Expect:`, as for a whole body (ADR-0023), so libcurl adds neither, and
  never waits for a 100 nor meets a 417.
- **The request's buffer holds 65,536 bytes:** a put waits while it holds that many or more, so
  it holds at most that plus the piece being put. A put copies its piece; the caller keeps it.
- **The read callable** gives the least of the room libcurl offers, the bytes buffered and the
  bytes `body-length` still owes, so no libcurl sends past the length (§2.1). With nothing
  buffered and no end, it marks the transfer paused for sending, and that libcurl has asked, in
  the hold that found the buffer empty, and answers `CURL_READFUNC_PAUSE`. At the end with
  everything given, it answers 0. At an end short of `body-length`, it records
  `body-length-mismatch` and answers `CURL_READFUNC_ABORT`, alike on every libcurl, where libcurl
  alone stalls or answers 26.
- **A put past `body-length`** is refused: the put raises `&curl-request-error`,
  `body-length-mismatch`, the piece not taken, and sets a refusal under the stream's mutex and
  queues a resume; the read callable, on the engine's thread, then records
  `body-length-mismatch` and answers `CURL_READFUNC_ABORT`, so the transfer fails with that
  reason. Once libcurl has read the length whole it calls the read callable no more, so the
  transfer's outcome reads the refusal too, closing the request stream in that hold: a put past
  the length that comes first fails the transfer, whatever libcurl answered, unless another
  reason was recorded first, and one that comes after answers `done` (ADR-0030, amended
  2026-10-31).
- **A put or an end** queues a resume when the transfer is paused for sending, once.
- **At most once** (ADR-0032, amended): `FRESH_CONNECT` at 1, owned. A seek callable and a
  prerequest callable, set on a streamed body alone, each answer OK while the read callable has
  handed libcurl no byte, a rewind then being a no-op, and the seek only for an offset of 0 from
  `SEEK_SET`; after that, each records `body-not-replayable` and refuses, the seek with
  `CURL_SEEKFUNC_CANTSEEK` and the prerequest callable at any attempt after the first with
  `CURL_PREREQFUNC_ABORT`. The recorded reason wins over 65, 56 and 42 (§3.1).
- **The prerequest callable is not redundant:** 8.5.0 resent a refused HTTP/2 stream with no
  seek in 4 runs of 8, and only the prerequest callable refused it (§5.1, as measured). Beside
  8.7.1's dropped rewind (§2.2), 7.85.0
  rewinds only once body bytes were sent, and drops on a resend the bytes it was handed but had
  not sent (`curl-7.85.0/lib/transfer.c:1910-1916`; the source reading, §5), so a refused HTTP/2
  stream in that window resends with no seek at all, and only the prerequest callable sees it.
- **A length of 0** sends `Content-Length: 0`; libcurl never calls the read callable, and a put
  is refused as past the length.
- **The callables' options are owned,** as `WRITEFUNCTION` and `WRITEDATA` are: `READFUNCTION`,
  `READDATA`, `HEADERFUNCTION`, `HEADERDATA`, `SEEKFUNCTION`, `SEEKDATA`, `PREREQFUNCTION` and
  `PREREQDATA`, beside `FRESH_CONNECT`; `test-unsupported`'s written-out table gains each.

### 3.4 The engine

- **Its turn gains two steps:** take, set up, cancel, **resume**, perform, read, **look**, poll.
  Resume comes after the cancels, so a cancel wins, and before the perform, which carries on what
  it resumed. The look reads the queue under the engine's mutex, and when a message is queued the
  turn skips its poll: Guix's 8.20.0 reads back, inside the perform, a wakeup made before it
  (§4.1, as measured; ADR-0012, amended 2026-10-30).
- **A resume names its token, never a handle,** and is carried out for a token still live, as a
  cancel is; one for a token taken out since is passed over, since the engine frees a transfer's
  easy handle in the turn it ends (ADR-0012, amended 2026-10-29).
- **The pause state lives under the stream's mutex:** a bit for each direction, and the one flag
  that a resume is queued. A callable sets its bit in the hold that found its buffer full or
  empty. The resume step, in one hold, clears both bits and the flag; then, holding no mutex and
  outside any critical section, it calls `curl_easy_pause` with `CURLPAUSE_CONT`, and each
  callable that still cannot go on pauses again, setting its bit, so a later look queues a new
  resume (§3.5). The prerequest callable clears the send bit at each attempt, since libcurl's own
  resend starts its attempt unpaused: on all five, its first read came within 1.165 ms of the
  second prerequest call, with no resume (§5.1, as measured).
- **`curl_easy_pause`'s answer** (ADR-0012, amended 2026-10-29): 0 goes on; any other fails the
  transfer, `pause-failed` with the code, unless a callable recorded a reason inside the call,
  which wins, and takes it out. So the refusal 7.85.0 and 8.5.0 lose in a replay fails the
  transfer there too (§2.1).
- **The lock order is the engine's mutex, then a stream's,** as the engine's, then a mailbox's.
  A look, a take or a put that queues a resume makes its stream hold and then its queueing hold
  one after the other, never nested, inside one `critical-section`, so an escape cannot fall
  between them and leave the flag set with nothing queued. The header callable publishes the
  head under the stream's mutex alone, as built; `head-wait` nests the engine's, then the
  stream's, and no path takes them the other way. The head has one writer, the engine's thread,
  which publishes it before it completes the token, so a reader that reads done before the head
  sees the head whenever there is one (slice 1's review). A
  look at the stream's end reads whether the token is done before it reads the buffer empty, or
  nests the two in that order, so the last piece is never mistaken for the end.
- **`curl_easy_pause` is bound `__collect_safe`,** through `bind-blocking`: it never blocks on
  the network (§2.2), but its replay of a gzip body under the hatch's `ACCEPT_ENCODING` ran up
  to 21.647 ms, and bound plain it held another thread's collection until it returned
  (§4.1, as measured).
- **No multiplexing on 8.7.0 and 8.7.1** (ADR-0030, amended 2026-10-29): there the engine sets
  `CURLMOPT_PIPELINING` to 0 on its multi handle as it makes it, so every HTTP/2 transfer has a
  connection of its own (§2.4). Slice 1 builds it and measures it on the Mac.
- **The paced poll** (ADR-0012, amended 2026-10-29): when a poll returns in under 1 ms while a
  transfer is paused, in a turn that took no message and in which no callable moved a byte, the
  engine sleeps 1 ms before its next poll. A counter the callables bump tells it.
- **The callables** (ADR-0013): the header, read, seek and prerequest callables, each
  `__collect_safe`, as `curl_multi_perform` and `curl_easy_pause` may fire them, locked where
  defined, handed the transfer's key, under a guard that records what is raised and answers what
  refuses the transfer: the header callable 0, the read callable `CURL_READFUNC_ABORT`, the seek
  callable `CURL_SEEKFUNC_CANTSEEK`, the prerequest callable `CURL_PREREQFUNC_ABORT`. None runs a
  procedure a caller supplied.
- **A planted fault** may name the resume step, as every step.

### 3.5 Takes, puts and the port

- **A take keeps what it takes until it returns:** inside its stream hold it copies the bytes,
  but leaves them in the stream; after the hold it removes them, the last thing before it
  returns, by writing fields only the taker writes, the queue's head and a count of bytes taken,
  the engine counting the buffer as the bytes appended less those taken. A Ctrl-C handler that
  escapes as the hold's critical section ends (§2.3) then leaves them for the next take, so none
  is lost; and none is given twice, so long as no event check falls between the removal and the
  return. `curl-token-next-piece` and the port's read do so alike.
- **And every look decides the resume,** not the take alone: each take, and each poll of
  `next-piece`'s and the port's waits, queues a resume when the transfer is paused for receiving,
  none is queued, and the buffer, as the taker counts it, is at or below half of
  `stream-buffer`. The write callable can pause between a take's hold and its removal, seeing the
  bytes still there, and a resume decided once is then never queued: the second review's model
  of this take ([`research/m6/review2/`](research/m6/review2/)) stalled 44, 45 and 55 times in
  50,000 pieces, 3 runs, two of them its own; deciding at every look, 0 in each, its looks queuing
  a resume a take alone would have missed 67, 27 and 38 times; a take that removes inside its
  hold, the control, 0.
- **A put commits inside its hold,** with its resume decision: committed after, it stalled 7, 11
  and 2 times in 50,000 pieces, and inside, 0 (the same directory). A handler that escapes as the
  hold ends leaves the put committed, so `curl-token-body-count : Token -> Natural`, the bytes
  committed, tells a caller that caught the escape what was taken.
- **Slice 1 proves the take** with the review's SIGINT probe as its test and the second
  review's model as its stress test, and sweeps a timer across the removal a tick at a time, as
  C10 did, with a planted control; slice 2 does the same for the put.
- **The port's read** copies from the piece at hand, or takes the next. With none, it waits, as
  every wait: a poll, sleeping between looks, refused inside a critical section, an assertion
  violation, before it waits.
- **At the end it answers 0 for a transfer that ended with a response,** every time after; for a
  failure, it raises the outcome's condition, every time after.
- **It belongs to the thread that made it:** a read that reaches `read!`, or a close, from
  another raises `&curl-wrong-thread`, since a close from another thread races a read (§2.3); a
  close that raises leaves the port open. Bytes Chez has already buffered are served to any
  thread without a call to `read!`: after the owner's `get-u8`, another thread's `get-u8`,
  `lookahead-u8` and `get-bytevector-n` returned them (slice 1's review), and Chez has no hook a
  buffered read passes through. Another thread's `get-bytevector-n`, `-n!` or `-all` that needs
  more than was buffered takes those bytes, then raises at `read!`, and loses them: the owner's
  next `get-u8` answered byte 128, after 127 buffered (slice 1's second review,
  [`research/m6/slice1-review2/`](research/m6/slice1-review2/)).
- **Its close cancels the token** if it is not done. A port no one closes is never closed: its
  transfer stays paused until its timeout or its engine's stop. The README says so.
- **`get-bytevector-n`, `-n!` and `-all` lose bytes when a read raises** the failure: earlier
  reads' bytes inside one call (§2.3). `get-bytevector-some`, `-some!` and `get-u8` do not. The
  README recommends `get-bytevector-some`, and a test pins each.

### 3.6 The high level

- **`call-with-response-port`** (ADR-0031): the call, as `http-request` takes it, its request
  with `stream-buffer`, then, at each head:
  - the drive reads the clocks, draws, and reads the head's bit for whether libcurl had asked for
    the body by the head (§3.2), once; the core answers whether the call ends with this response
    or steps past it, by the rules `exchange-step` applies with those values: a redirect it
    follows, a status a retry takes. That answer is a pure predicate;
  - a hop's body is read whole, through `curl-token-next-piece`, up to `body-limit`, then stepped
    by `exchange-step` with the same values the predicate read, so the two cannot disagree, into
    the history. A failure while the hop's body is read is stepped with clocks read at the
    failure, so a 28 at the bound is the bound's (the M5 plan, §3.5);
  - a retry's wait is measured from the head's time, not from the hop's end;
  - the final head's response and the port go to PROC, once. A call that ends with a response
    whose body it read whole, a retried status when the time ran out among them, hands PROC that
    response and a port over its body in memory.
- **`next-request` carries the stream's keys:** each hop's request keeps `stream-buffer`, and,
  with the body, `body` `stream` and `body-length`; a hop that drops the body, a `post` through a
  301 or 302 and any method through a 303, drops both. `body-not-replayable` is checked first.
- **When PROC returns or escapes,** the port is closed, which cancels a live transfer, by a
  critical wind, as `drive-exchange`'s after thunk cancels; the call answers PROC's values.
- **The port PROC reads is the high level's own,** over `curl-token-next-piece`. Its read raises
  the low level's condition compounded with the call's `&http-exchange`, and with `&http-timeout`
  when the 28 was the call's bound (the M5 plan, §3.3).
- **A producer** (ADR-0031) is called on the caller's thread when `curl-token-room-wait` answers
  `#t`, so never before libcurl first asks: a call that fails before its body was asked for, a
  refused connect among them, stays retryable. It is never called after it answered the eof
  object. The drive holds the one piece it answered until it is put, and checks it against
  `body-length` before the put, so no guard around the put is needed.
- **The producer is fed wherever the caller's thread waits:** in the drive's wait for the head,
  room or the end, in a hop's whole read, and in the port's read when no piece is ready. So a
  server that answers before the body is done, a full-duplex one, or a 413 or a 303 while the read
  is paused, is never left waiting for a body no one feeds; §2.1 found 7.85.0 does not end such a
  transfer until the body resumes. A PROC that never reads, and never returns, starves its
  upload; the README says so.
- **But a hop whose body libcurl had not asked for by its head is never fed:** its producer is
  still unused, and its next request may need it whole. Slice 3 measures, on the five, whether
  libcurl asks for the body after a final head of 300 or more that came before its first ask;
  where it does, the hop's transfer is cancelled once its buffered pieces are read, and its
  history entry holds what arrived.
- **The producer's answer** that is no bytevector of a byte or more nor the eof object is an
  assertion violation from the call, once its transfer is cancelled. The producer's raise leaves
  the call as raised, once its transfer is cancelled. Inside the port's read it is sticky: every
  later read raises it again, and the call raises it once PROC returns, so a PROC that catches it
  cannot make a cut upload succeed. A producer that reads the port it feeds is an assertion
  violation, a flag guarding the read.
- **The drive's waits are bounded polls, unguarded,** each then reading what is ready as data
  (ADR-0030, decision 4).
- **At most once** (ADR-0032, amended): a call counts its producer as called once libcurl had
  asked for the body by a head, as the head's bit says, whether or not the drive had fed it yet.
  No retry takes such a call. A redirect whose next request keeps the body, as `body-dropped?`
  decides, raises `&http-redirect-error`, `body-not-replayable`: a 307 or 308, and a 301 or 302
  of every method but `post`.
- **The bound** (ADR-0031): each transfer's `timeout-ms` is the time left, PROC's reading
  included.
- **A group takes no producer:** `go` and `http-parallel` with a producer as `body` raise
  `&http-request-error`, `stream-in-group`, before anything is submitted (§9).

### 3.8 The seam and the conventions

- **check-seam:** the four new callables are `(curl private transfer)`'s, where
  `foreign-callable` alone may be, each the whole right-hand side of a `define` (ADR-0013).
  `curl_easy_pause` is native's, through `check-owner`. `make-custom-binary-input-port` is
  R6RS's. The high level needs no new Chez name.
- **test-constants** gains `curl_easy_pause`'s prototype and the callables' typedefs;
  **test-collect-safe** their rows.
- **The counters gate:** a stream makes nothing native beyond its transfer: its buffers are
  bytevectors, and the read callable copies by the checked copy.
- **The memory gate** runs the three new suites, as every suite.
- **Every raw-face check makes its own engine** (ADR-0020).
- **The thread rule's test** (brief §9, risk 2; ADR-0012, decision 4): inside a consumer of
  pieces and of the port, a producer, and PROC, the thread is never the engine's, and the chunk
  log's threads are the engine's alone. It is watched failing by a mutant whose write callable
  hands each chunk it takes to the token's callback (ADR-0021), on the engine's thread, the
  suite's consumer being that callback. The high level's half holds by construction, as in M5: a
  request holds no procedure (ADR-0023), so no path runs the producer or PROC on the engine's
  thread.
