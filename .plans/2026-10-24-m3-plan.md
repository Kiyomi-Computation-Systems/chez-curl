# M3 plan: the engine

- **Date:** 2026-10-24
- **Status:** draft. In §3, a point citing a 2026-10-24 amendment or ADR-0019 was decided by
  the maintainer. The rest is this plan's design, which a slice's measurements and review may
  revise. Each slice starts with the measurements it lists.
- **Scope:** brief §8's M3, spike E1 (brief §5), and what the decision records hand M3
- **Records:** [ADR-0002](decisions/0002-runtime-libcurl-discovery.md) (amended 2026-10-24) ·
  [ADR-0008](decisions/0008-curl-url-only.md) (amended 2026-10-24) ·
  [ADR-0009](decisions/0009-typed-escape-hatch.md) (amended 2026-10-24) ·
  [ADR-0012](decisions/0012-engine-thread-owns-every-handle.md) (amended 2026-10-24) ·
  [ADR-0013](decisions/0013-collect-safe-blocking-calls.md) (amended 2026-10-24, accepted
  from E1 the same day) · [ADR-0014](decisions/0014-valgrind-memory-gate.md) ·
  [ADR-0019](decisions/0019-a-stalled-lookup-does-not-stall-the-engine.md) (new) ·
  [global-constraints](global-constraints.md)

M3 moves every transfer onto an engine. An engine is one Chez thread that owns a multi handle,
waits in `curl_multi_poll`, and runs the transfers other threads submit. A caller submits a
request and gets a token. It can await the token's completion, cancel it, or stop the engine.

- **M3 adds nothing public.** `(curl)`'s exports do not change. The engine is
  `(curl private engine)`, and M4 builds the public records and the asynchronous API on it
  (brief §8).
- **M2's blocking path retires.** ADR-0012 says that from M3 on, only engines own transfer
  handles. So `perform-transfer` and the `curl_easy_perform` binding go. The request, its
  checks, its settings, the write callable and the response stay in
  `(curl private transfer)`, and the engine reuses them, as the M2 plan said it would.
  `test-transfer` and `test-transfer-tls` run every scenario through an engine.
- **Spike E1 comes first** (slice 1). It reruns §2's measurements from committed programs,
  and accepts or amends ADR-0013 before anything is bound.

## 2. Measured before planning (2026-10-24)

Scratch programs under `build/verify/m3/measure/` (not kept: E1 commits its own).
- **In the container:** Chez 10.4.0, libcurl 8.20.0 with GnuTLS.
- **In Docker `ubuntu:24.04`, under the CI stand-in's Chez 10.4.0:** the floor's 7.85.0, and
  Ubuntu's 8.5.0 with OpenSSL and with GnuTLS.
- **Chez's source:** Guix's 10.4.0 checkout.

- **A plain wait stalls other threads' collections up to about 500 times its length.** A worker
  allocated beside an engine thread waiting, idle, in `curl_multi_poll`:

  | Engine | Worker's allocation, 11 collections |
  |---|---|
  | none | 13.9–14.6 ms |
  | `__collect_safe` poll, 300 ms | 14.1–15.5 ms |
  | plain poll, 1 ms | 5.3–5.4 s |
  | plain poll, 3 ms | 15.9 s |
  | plain poll, 10 ms | 54.4 s |
  | plain poll, 300 ms | never finished: killed at 30, 60 or 120 s, 6 runs of 6; extrapolates to about 27 min |

  The cause is in Chez:
  - a thread checks for a pending event only when its event counter runs out, and
    `default-timer-ticks` is 1000 (`s/cmacros.ss`);
  - the loop spends about two ticks per poll, so it checks about every 500 polls, and a
    collection waits for the next check. Close together, as here, each waited about 460
    (spike E1 measured 459, a lone one 51 to 466, and showed the ticks at work:
    [findings](research/e1/findings.md), §1);
  - a `__collect_safe` call deactivates the thread, so a collection need not wait for it.
  
  The rows for no engine, the safe poll and the plain 1 ms poll held on all four Linux
  libcurls. The others ran on 8.20.0.
- **`curl_multi_wakeup` is race-free for a queue.**
  - A wakeup reached a waiting poll in 0.03–0.16 ms.
  - One sent while no poll ran made the next poll return at once.
  - Two coalesced into one.

  The same on 8.20.0, twice, and once each on the other three. So "queue under the mutex,
  then wake" loses no message, whenever it lands.
- **Callables:**
  - **A `__collect_safe` callable may be entered from a plain call.** Its entry saves the
    thread's state from `S_activate_thread`, and its exit restores exactly that, so in an
    active thread both are no-ops (`c/thread.c`, and `s/x86_64.ss` and `s/arm64.ss` alike).
  - **Every byte arrived,** on 8.20.0. Four transfers on one multi handle, among them two
    drips, ran while another thread collected about 1,000 times a run. That held 3 runs of 3
    for each of three pairings: a `__collect_safe` callable from a plain
    `curl_multi_perform`, both plain, and both `__collect_safe`.
  - **Write callbacks fired inside `curl_multi_perform` only:** 352 of 352, none inside the
    poll.
- **Ctrl-C** (ADR-0012, amended):
  - Chez delivers it to the main thread alone, and drops it in any other.
  - A caller in `condition-wait` answered it only when the wait ended, at 3,000 ms, even
    with timed waits of 100 ms in a loop.
  - Timed waits each inside their own `critical-section` answered at 1,100 ms, 2 runs of 2:
    leaving a critical section runs `$event` at once.
  - A `__collect_safe` poll was cut short by the signal, at 1,024 ms.
- **The load race reproduces.** Chez locks no library's invocation (`s/syntax.ss`). Two
  threads making their first reference to `(curl private native)` together raised
  `cyclic dependency` on the second, 10 runs of 10 (ADR-0002, amended).
- **A stalled name lookup blocks teardown, and a timeout, for 9 to 10 s** (ADR-0019):
  - `curl_multi_remove_handle` on 7.85.0 and 8.5.0;
  - one `curl_multi_perform` call, at a transfer's own timeout, on the same libcurls;
  - `curl_multi_cleanup` on 8.20.0.

  `QUICK_EXIT` removes the first two on 8.5.0, and `CURLMOPT_QUICK_EXIT` the third on
  8.20.0. The floor has neither. The container cannot stall a lookup: it has no
  `/etc/resolv.conf`, so the lookup fails in 1 ms, and port 53 is refused to it.
- **Every recorded libcurl has `AsynchDNS`,** so none resolves inside `curl_multi_perform`
  itself.

## 3. Decisions that bind every slice

### 3.1 The engine's shape (ADR-0012)

- **`make-engine` loads libcurl on the caller's thread first,** by referencing it, and only
  then forks the engine's thread (ADR-0002, amended). The thread makes the multi handle. It
  reports back that it is ready, or the refusal that stopped it, and `make-engine` returns
  the engine or raises that refusal. As built, it is `make-curl-engine`: Chez's own
  `make-engine` is its engines, which tests import.
- **Messages are submit, cancel and stop.** Each is queued under the engine's mutex, and then
  `curl_multi_wakeup` wakes the poll. That is the only libcurl call a caller's thread makes
  on an engine's handle.
- **The loop, on the engine's thread:**
  1. take the queue;
  2. set up each submitted transfer and add it;
  3. carry out each cancel, then a stop;
  4. `curl_multi_perform`;
  5. read each finished transfer with `curl_multi_info_read`, read its response, take it
     apart and complete its token;
  6. `curl_multi_poll`, for at most 1000 ms. libcurl shortens the wait to its own timers.
- **Every native object a transfer uses is made, used and freed on the engine's thread.**
  Each make-and-record, and each free-and-unrecord, is one critical section (ADR-0013,
  amended 2026-10-23).
- **Concurrency costs no threads:** one engine thread runs every transfer submitted to it.
  An engine never starts another thread, and `fork-thread` belongs to
  `(curl private engine)` alone (§3.9).

### 3.2 The loop never waits in a plain call (ADR-0013, ADR-0019)

- **Through `bind-blocking`:**
  - `curl_multi_poll`;
  - `curl_multi_perform`;
  - `curl_multi_remove_handle`;
  - `curl_multi_cleanup`.
- **Plain:**
  - `curl_multi_init`, `curl_multi_add_handle`, `curl_multi_wakeup`, `curl_multi_info_read`
    and `curl_multi_strerror`;
  - `curl_easy_cleanup` after removal, which measured 0 ms in every case, and E1 classed as
    plain.
- **A test holds it by behaviour, not only by the conventions table.** A worker's collections
  beside an idle engine must finish within a bound, in a bounded child. With the poll plain,
  §2 says each would take up to about 500 s, so the child is killed and the check fails by
  name. It asks for collections back to back: a lone one can wait far less (spike E1).

### 3.3 One check for the thread rule (ADR-0012, decision 3)

- **native records which thread made each easy, multi and `CURLU` handle and each string
  list.** It is recorded when the handle is made, in the same critical section as its count.
- **Every native procedure that takes a handle compares `get-thread-id` with that thread
  first,** and a mismatch raises `&curl-wrong-thread`, carrying who, expected and actual. So
  the check is one check, in the library every handle operation already goes through. It
  holds for the load's header probe, and for the URL check on a caller's thread, as much as
  for the engine.
- **The exceptions are the calls that touch no handle** (ADR-0012), and `curl_multi_wakeup`,
  which may be called from any thread.
- **The cost:** a table lookup under a mutex per handle operation. Slice 2 measures it.
- **The test:** a handle an engine made, used from another thread, raises with all three
  fields. Without the check, that call goes through, so the test has been watched failing.

### 3.4 Submitting and awaiting

- **Submit runs on the caller's thread.** It takes the request's snapshot (M2's rules),
  checks its URL and its seam options there (ADR-0008, amended), and queues the snapshot.
  Every refusal raises at submit, before anything is queued. A submit to a stopped or failed
  engine raises `&curl-engine-error`.
- **A token holds one outcome:** the response, or the condition the transfer failed with.
  `cancelled` and `stopped` are `&curl-transfer-error` reasons. M4 owns the full taxonomy of
  `CURLE_*` codes as conditions.
- **Await returns the response, or raises the outcome, on the awaiting thread.** Any thread
  may await a token, any number of times, and the outcome is kept.
- **Await answers Ctrl-C.** It polls: each read of the token is a short hold through
  `locked`, and between reads it sleeps, from 50 µs doubling to 10 ms, holding nothing, so a
  keyboard interrupt runs as the sleep ends. A wait inside a critical section, as planned
  first, held every other thread's collections until it ended, and a wait whose mutex a
  critical `dynamic-wind` held, chosen next, deadlocked with a collection. Slice 2 measured
  both, and the maintainer chose this (ADR-0013, amended 2026-10-25).
- **An escape from await leaves the request running.** Await again returns its outcome. A
  caller that wants it stopped cancels it: the low level has no policy, and M5's request group
  will cancel what it leaves.

### 3.5 Cancel and stop (ADR-0012, amended)

- **Cancel is a message, and returns at once.** It affects a token in one of three states:
  - **queued:** it completes as `cancelled`, and nothing native is made. As built, this one
    is no message: the caller's thread completes the token itself, under the engine's
    mutex, and the engine passes over a done token's submit;
  - **live:** the engine removes it, takes it apart, and completes it as `cancelled`;
  - **done:** nothing changes.
- **Stop is a message.** The engine:
  - completes every queued token as `stopped`;
  - cancels every live one, as `stopped`;
  - frees all it holds and cleans up the multi handle;
  - signals the stop as its thread's last act.

  The caller's stop returns once that signal arrives. A later stop returns at once.

### 3.6 No caller code on the engine's thread (ADR-0012, decision 4)

- **The write callable runs no procedure a caller supplied.** So the chunk hook goes.
  - Tests that watched chunks arrive read a list the callable records instead: each chunk's
    length and the thread it ran on, kept with the transfer as data.
  - A fault the callable must survive, such as a raise inside its guard, is planted as data:
    a private field naming the chunk where it raises. It is never a procedure.
- **The request holds no procedure,** and making a request with one is refused.
- **M2's escape machinery has nothing left to guard, and goes with `perform-transfer`.**
  That is the performing mark, and the hook's `dynamic-wind`. The engine's thread runs no
  caller code. Chez sends Ctrl-C to the main thread alone, and the engine sets no timer.
- **A limit, recorded:** `collect-request-handler` is process-wide, so a caller's handler that
  escapes could run on the engine's thread, inside the callable.
- **The test ADR-0012 asks for.** The callable's recorded thread is the engine's for every
  chunk, and await returns on the awaiting thread. A request carrying a procedure is refused
  when it is made. M4's completion callbacks will add the delivery half of the test.

### 3.7 A raise inside the engine

- **The loop runs under a guard.** A raise there happens between libcurl calls, never inside
  one, since the callable's own guard keeps raises in.
- **The engine fails cleanly:**
  - every live and queued token completes with `&curl-engine-error`, reason `failed`,
    carrying the condition;
  - the engine frees all it holds, and marks itself failed;
  - a later submit raises, and stop returns at once.
- **A private fault, planted as data,** raises at a chosen step of the loop, so the test has
  something to fail on.

### 3.8 Counters (ADR-0014, decision 7)

- **The process counters stay the gate.** They gain a `multi` kind.
- **Decision 7's "per engine" becomes an engine's table of live transfers,** which stop
  empties, and checks empty before the thread signals. A suite then asserts the process
  counts are zero after stop, as every suite already does at its end. ADR-0014 is amended
  when slice 2 builds it.

### 3.9 The seam's and the conventions' reviewed additions

- **check-seam** (ADR-0007):
  - `allowed-chez-names` gains `fork-thread`, `get-thread-id`, `make-condition`,
    `condition-wait`, `condition-broadcast` and `make-time`;
  - `library-only-chez-names` gives `fork-thread` to `(curl private engine)` alone.
- **foreign-conventions:**
  - `curl_multi_perform`'s row moves from plain to `__collect_safe`, as the E1 finding it
    quotes says. It, `curl_multi_remove_handle`, `curl_multi_cleanup` and `curl_multi_poll`
    are `(bound-by bind-blocking)`;
  - the other multi entry points get plain rows;
  - `curl_easy_perform`'s row and binding go;
  - `curl_multi_setopt`'s variadic row exists already, and slice 3 binds it.
- **test-constants:** a prototype row for each new entry point, the `CURLMsg` layout, and
  the new constants.

## 5. Slice 2: the engine

### 5.1 First, measure

- **The thread check's cost per handle operation,** against a transfer's own time.
- **The engine under the gate's Valgrind.** Valgrind runs one thread at a time, so a busy
  thread starves the rest (`traps.md`). Measure whether the loop's waits in `curl_multi_poll`
  leave the suites' threads their turn, and pace any test that collects or races.
- **The ordering of completions read from `curl_multi_info_read`** beside cancels, on
  8.20.0 and the floor. For example, a transfer that finishes in the same perform as its
  cancel arrives.

### 5.2 What it builds

- **native:**
  - the multi bindings (§3.2);
  - each handle's owning thread (§3.3);
  - the multi handle, counted as `multi`;
  - `curl_easy_perform` removed.
- **layouts:** `CURLMsg`. `data` is a union of a pointer and a `CURLcode`, and the result is
  read as an `int` at the union's offset. Its row names the C field `data.result`.
- **constants:** `CURLM_OK`, `CURLM_UNKNOWN_OPTION` and `CURLMSG_DONE`, each with a row in
  `test-constants`.
- **conditions:** `&curl-engine-error`, with reasons `stopped` and `failed`.
- **transfer:**
  - setting up an easy handle from a snapshot, and reading its response, become procedures
    the engine calls on its own thread;
  - the snapshot, the field table, the pins and the TLS settings are unchanged;
  - `perform-transfer` and the chunk hook go;
  - `perform-transfer-with`, the test seam, becomes a submit with the same rules (ADR-0009,
    amended).
- **engine:** §3.1 to §3.8.
- **The suites:**
  - `test-transfer` and `test-transfer-tls` submit each scenario to an engine and await it,
    with their tables and version rows unchanged. A scenario that watched chunks reads the
    recorded list (§3.6);
  - `test-engine` holds the rest.

### 5.3 `test-engine`

Each check is watched failing on a mutant before it counts.

- **Concurrency:**
  - many transfers on one engine, their drips overlapping in time, every chunk on the
    engine's thread;
  - two caller threads awaiting together.
- **Submit:** each refusal raised at submit, with nothing queued and the counts unchanged.
- **Cancel:** in each of its three states, and racing a completion.
- **Stop:**
  - with transfers queued and live, each completing as `stopped`;
  - a later submit refused, and a second stop;
  - counts at zero after it.
- **The thread rule:** §3.3's raise, with who, expected and actual.
- **The collection bound,** in a bounded child (§3.2).
- **Ctrl-C:** a child whose main thread awaits a slow transfer gets SIGINT, and its handler
  runs within a bound (§3.4). The child's own counts are printed and asserted zero.
- **The load:** a child whose first reference to the library is `make-engine`, run many
  times, never meets `cyclic dependency`. Dropped as built: a top-level program runs the load
  before its first line, so the child passed with the early reference removed, 20 runs of 20
  (ADR-0002, amended 2026-10-25).
- **A raise inside the loop** (§3.7).
- **The memory gate:** `make test-memory` runs `test-engine` like any suite.

### 5.4 Deleting M2's blocking path

Deleting a subsystem disarms the checks built around it (`traps.md`). So for each check that
guarded `perform-transfer`, the slice's report says what would make it fail now, or why it
retires:
- the escape sweep, and its child, `transfer-escapes`;
- the performing mark's tests;
- the hook's refusals;
- the rows that quote ADR-0013's 2026-10-23 amendment.

The records that describe M2's escapes are amended to say that they ended with M2's path:
ADR-0013 (2026-10-23), global-constraints and traps.md.

## 6. Slice 3: stalled lookups (ADR-0019)

### 6.1 First, measure

- **Whether a stand-in `getaddrinfo` under `LD_PRELOAD` stalls libcurl's resolver thread,**
  on each of the four Linux libcurls. It defers every other name to the real one through
  `dlsym(RTLD_NEXT, ...)`. It must reproduce §2's teardown and timeout tables before any test
  rests on it.
- **What it takes on the Mac:** `DYLD_INSERT_LIBRARIES`, and whether Apple's libcurl resolves
  through `getaddrinfo` at all. That is measured there, or recorded as unmeasured.

As measured (ADR-0019 and ADR-0001, amended 2026-10-25;
[research](research/stall/)): the stand-in reproduced §2's tables on the four Linux libcurls,
each wait scaled to its stall. And 7.85.0 and 8.5.0 answer no long multi option by its
value, so `MAX_TOTAL_CONNECTIONS`, which serialises two transfers at 1, shows a long reaching
`curl_multi_setopt`. The maintainer chose to run the stall checks on Linux alone, with a probe
for the Mac, and to watch `curl_multi_setopt` fail on Apple arm64 by that behaviour.

### 6.2 What it builds

- **`QUICK_EXIT` 1 among an engine transfer's pinned settings,** where the loaded libcurl
  knows it. The settings are computed as data and held whole, as the TLS settings are.
- **`curl_multi_setopt`, bound once for a long through `bind-variadic`,** and
  `CURLMOPT_QUICK_EXIT` 1 set when an engine is made. `CURLM_UNKNOWN_OPTION` is accepted.
- **`CURLMOPT_QUICK_EXIT` in constants, checked where the headers have it.** It is an enum
  member, which the preprocessor cannot test for. So the generated C program reads it under
  `#if LIBCURL_VERSION_NUM >= 0x081400`, and against older headers the suite names it as
  unchecked there. A constant newer than the floor carries the version that added it,
  written out in the suite. Its value cannot change once released, so the container's
  check holds it.
- **The stall child,** on Linux, under the stand-in:
  - a cancel during a stalled lookup returns within a bound, while another transfer on the
    same engine completes;
  - a timeout of 500 ms completes as 28 within a bound;
  - stop returns within a bound.

  Without a control, the bound proves nothing:
  - on 8.5.0, a private switch turns `QUICK_EXIT` off, and the child must then show the
    stall;
  - on 8.20.0, the switch turns the multi option off, and stop must then show it;
  - on the floor, which lacks `QUICK_EXIT`, the child asserts the stall itself, the
    recorded limit (ADR-0019).

  As built (ADR-0019, amended 2026-10-25): the switch is
  `make-curl-engine-without-quick-exit`; each event is judged against the stand-in's log,
  not a clock; the verdicts are a row per libcurl version in `test-engine`; and the child
  counts the transfers that set `QUICK_EXIT`, so the switch is held by what the engine did.
- **`test-variadic`'s two drips,** through `curl-multi-setopt-long`, and `check-variadic`'s
  sixth mutant, that use alone through `bind-required` (ADR-0001, amended 2026-10-25).

### 6.3 The Mac

`make check-variadic` runs there, since `curl_multi_setopt` is a new variadic binding
(ADR-0001). Its multi mutant must fail `test-variadic`'s drips by name. Before it, `make test`
prints the 8.7.1 rows `test-variadic`'s `minus-one-rows` and `test-engine`'s
`reuse-verdicts` need (ADR-0020, amended 2026-10-26). And the Mac runs the stall
probe, `.plans/research/stall/run.sh mac lookup multi-setopt maxconn`, whose answer goes
into ADR-0019.
